ABSTRACT
Feature selection algorithms are crucial technologies for reducing the dimensionality of high-dimensional data.
However, the exponential expansion of the decision space due to high-dimensional data makes most feature
selection algorithms highly susceptible to local optima. To increase the quality of feature selection, we propose a
dynamic multi-swarm whale optimization algorithm based on elite tuning (EMSWOA). First, we propose a dy-
namic multiple swarm construction mechanism based on the centroid distance metric, which enhances the
exploitation and exploration abilities of the algorithm by improving the connectivity between sub-population
topologies and preventing the algorithm from falling into precocious convergence. Secondly, to address the
issue of invalid probability flips caused by random thresholds during feature space transformation, we design a
novel elite tuning mechanism. This mechanism uses the high-quality information in the elite solution to assess
and correct the probability of feature flipping; it effectively improves the algorithm ’ s recognition of important
features and reduces the interference of invalid probability flips in the optimization search. Finally, we validated
the EMSWOA against six comparison algorithms on 19 datasets. The experimental results demonstrate that,
compared with the most effective benchmark algorithms, the EMSWOA yields an average accuracy increase of
2.91 % and an average reduction in the number of features by a factor of 3.58.
1. Introduction
With the advent of the Big Data era, an increasing amount of high-
dimensional data are appearing in everyday life. However, the high-
dimensional data collected contain considerable redundant or irrele-
vant information, which not only increases the computational overhead
of the model, but also causes degradation in model performance due to
problems such as over-fitting [1] . Feature Selection (FS) is an effective
dimensionality reduction method that reduces the complexity of fea-
tures, decreases computational overhead, and enhances accuracy by
selecting the optimal subset from high-dimensional feature data,
thereby eliminating redundant and irrelevant features [2] . As a key
technique for data preprocessing, feature selection algorithms are
widely used in numerous practical problems such as neural data analysis

Feature selection algorithms include filter-based approaches,
wrapper-based approaches and embedded approaches [4] . Filter-based
algorithms usually filter a subset by the relevance criterion of the fea-
tures, and therefore have a low computational overhead [5] . As the
evaluation criteria of filter-based algorithms are not related to the
learning process of the algorithm, the accuracy of the selected subset
performs poorly. Wrapper-based feature selection algorithms, however,
generally treat the feature selection process as a search problem. They
identify candidate feature subsets using methods such as heuristic
searches and directly employ classifier performance metrics to evaluate
the merits of these subsets. The optimal feature subset is obtained by
integrating search algorithms with classifiers trained during the process.
This type of method, taking into account the correlations among fea-
tures, imposes higher demands on the global optimization capabilities of
search algorithms [6] . Additionally, embedded feature selection
methods integrate the feature selection process into the model training,
leveraging the model ’ s inherent mechanisms to automatically perform
feature selection through model parameters or structures. However, the
effectiveness of feature selection is directly dependent on the choice of
model, potentially constrained by the model ’ s own performance [7] .
Although feature selection algorithms are continuously evolving,
most methods still face numerous challenges when performing classifi-
cation tasks on high-dimensional datasets [8] . Consequently, the task of
high-dimensional feature selection remains a focal point of research. The
FS problem is essentially a combinatorial optimization issue [9] . The
goal of the FS is to effectively remove redundant and irrelevant features
to obtain a higher classification accuracy with a minimum number of
features. Metaheuristic algorithms have shown outstanding capabilities
for addressing combinatorial optimization issues and have been suc-
cessful in recent years for combinatorial optimization problems such as
data clustering [10,11] . Benefiting from the global optimization capa-
bilities of the meta-heuristic algorithm, it has also gained significant
attention. In contrast to approaches such as exhaustive searching [12] ,
metaheuristics do not rely on a priori knowledge of the domain or
specific assumptions about the search space, making them more suitable
for feature selection tasks [13] .
The whale optimization algorithm (WOA) [14] has received much
attention as a representative algorithm in the field of metaheuristic al-
gorithms in recent years. The WOA is suitable for solving complex
feature selection problems in the search space because of its strong
global optimization capability [15] and flexible search direction control
mechanism. Specifically, the vector representation of individual whales
in the population is consistent with the size of the feature subset of the
selected features. In the WOA, the feeding process of whales is the
random search process of constructing a subset of features in feature
selection. Individual whales with better fitness values during WOA
predation were considered more important for the selected traits. A
subset of the more optimal features was obtained through the flexible
foraging process of the whales. In addition, more importantly, due to the
shrinking encircling mechanism and spiral updating position of the
WOA algorithm, the WOA algorithm can more flexibly control search
direction in complex space; thus, it can be better realized in complex
space spatial exploitation and exploration. Therefore, the WOA algo-
rithm may be more suitable for solving the feature selection problem.
WOA algorithms have been tested on high-dimensional and large-scale
optimization problems. For example, Zhang et al. [16] proposed a
hybrid WOA based on aggregation strategies for high-dimensional
problems.
However, the above WOA-based feature algorithms, which are
dominated by single populations [17] , are still unable to overcome the
problem of insufficient exploration capability of high-dimensional
complex solution spaces [18] . Due to the computational time con-
straints inherent in the single-swarm Whale Optimization Algorithm
(WOA) for feature selection, it is challenging to conduct unlimited
searches and iterations over feature subsets. Consequently, the
single-population WOA typically explores only a small part of the de-
cision space, which significantly restricts the acquisition of the optimal
subset and adversely impacts the quality of the final feature selection.
Therefore, it is crucial to improve the search capability of WOA algo-
rithms in high-dimensional feature spaces. Multi-swarm optimization
algorithms can divide the entire population into multiple sub-swarms,
which learn and update in their own search spaces. This helps to
improve the diversity of the population, increase the exploration ability,
and can effectively avoid premature convergence. For example, Yang
et al. [19] proposed an evolutionary state-driven multi-population
cooperative particle swarm optimization algorithm for complex opti-
mization problems.
In addition, none of the above feature selection algorithms have been
improved for the invalid probability flipping problem. This is because
existing metaheuristic feature selection algorithms employ trans-
formation functions that are usually of two types, S-type or V-type [3] .
By applying the transformation function to the update step size in each
iteration cycle, the corresponding probability value for each dimension
is obtained. This probability is also used as a measure of feature
importance, and the feature is flipped or selected based on a comparison
of the probability value with a threshold. Therefore, setting the
threshold is crucial for determining which features are selected. There
are two existing methods for setting the threshold: a fixed threshold
based on a constant value and a dynamic threshold based on random
values. Using a constant value as the threshold is not only make it
difficult to determine the optimal setting but also results in a lack
generalizability for different datasets. For example, Yousri et al. [20]
used a fixed threshold in their marine predator feature selection algo-
rithm. Using a dynamic threshold for feature importance measurement
can increase the algorithm ’ s perturbation, but there is also a problem of
invalid probability flipping due to the randomness of the dynamic
threshold; for example, in Abualigah et al. ’ s [21] used a random
threshold for feature measurement. Invalid probability flipping can
reduce the selection probability of high-quality features, thereby
affecting the search speed and quality of the best subset.
To overcome the insufficient detection ability of the WOA algorithm
in high-dimensional complex solution space and the problem of invalid
probability flipping when features are mapped discretely, we propose a
dynamic multi-swarm WOA based on elite tuning (EMSWOA). Building
on the foundation of the WOA algorithm, we introduce an elite correc-
tion strategy to alleviate the problem of invalid probability flipping and
devise a dynamic multi-swarm construction mechanism based on
centroid distance to enhance the global optimization ability of the
EMSWOA and improve the quality of feature subsets. Our innovations
are as follows:
• A dynamic multi-swarm strategy based on centroid distance was
proposed, which uses centroid measurements to directionally guide
the dynamic formation of subpopulations, enhancing their exploi-
tation capabilities.
• A dynamic population recombination mechanism with a linearly
decreasing number of subpopulations was developed to increase the
algorithm ’ s independence in early search stages within the feature
space and enhance information exchange and global exploration
capabilities in later iterations.
• A local sparsity metric mechanism was introduced, utilizing a
comprehensive assessment of individual congestion and dispersion
in the feature space to maintain the diversity of candidate solutions,
thereby increasing the probability of identifying the optimal feature
subset.
• An elite tuning strategy is proposed to use two generations of elite
individuals to correct the probabilistic invalid flip problem caused by
random thresholds and to avoid the performance drain and distur-
bance caused by invalid probabilistic flips.

2.3. Literature review of feature selection methods
Based on the feature selection process, feature selection algorithms
can be categorized into evolutionary computation (EC) class methods
and non-evolutionary computation (NON-EC) class methods. This sec-
tion provides a literature review of these two categories of feature se-
lection algorithms.
2.3.1. EC-based feature selection
Due to the strong global optimization capabilities of EC algorithms,
EC-based feature selection methods have long been among the
commonly used approaches for feature dimensionality reduction [22].
However, given the exponential growth in feature space complexity due
to high-dimensional data, exploring diversity in EC feature selection
methods is highly important. One line of research enhances the global
optimization capability of EC algorithms to improve their accuracy in
feature selection [23–26]. For instance, Hu et al. [23] introduced an
enhanced black widow optimization algorithm for feature selection by
designing new spider mate and mutation strategies. Mohamed et al. [24]
enhanced the capabilities of feature selection by integrating a chaos
optimization strategy with multiple optimization mechanisms, propos-
ing a binary-enhanced Spider Wasp Optimizer. Nonetheless, due to the
complexity of high-dimensional feature spaces, methods that merely
improve the optimization capabilities of EC algorithms still risk
converging to local optima. Some scholars perform feature selection on
high-dimensional data by combining filter and EC algorithms. For
example, Chen et al. [5] et al. used ReliefF algorithm for initial filtering
of high dimensional features followed by particle swarm optimization
algorithm for dimensionality reduction of high dimensional features
through multi-task information sharing. Askr et al. [27] improved the
golden jackal optimization algorithm by combining Copula entropy and
four enhancement strategies, achieving dimensionality reduction of
features. Pan et al. [28] utilized the ReliefF algorithm and Copula en-
tropy simultaneously to enhance the quality of the initial population,
and combined this with an improved Gray Wolf Optimization algorithm
to select features from high-dimensional data. However, this hybrid
approach to multi-type feature selection increases the complexity of the
algorithm and the initial use of a filter-based feature selection method
may result in the loss of diversity within the feature subsets. Some
scholars have also recognized the potential of multi-swarm structures in
high-dimensional feature selection tasks. For instance, Braik et al. [29]
proposed an adaptive dynamic elite opposition-based Ali Baba and the
forty thieves algorithm, which divides the entire population into two
sub-populations to maintain diversity within the population. However, a
smaller number of sub-populations might not effectively cover the
extensive high-dimensional feature space. To further enhance the
extensive exploration of high-dimensional feature spaces, Li et al. [30]
introduced a decomposition-based multi-population evolutionary algo-
rithm. This method involves a multi-population generation strategy
based on feature weights, which improves the global exploration capa-
bility of the algorithm. Nevertheless, the fixed number of
sub-populations, being a hyperparameter that does not adapt dynami-
cally during the optimization process, limits the further enhancement of
feature selection capabilities.
The WOA as one of the representative algorithm in the EC domain,
has been increasingly utilized in the feature selection arena. Recent
literature on WOA-based feature selection algorithms is catalogued in
Table 1. These algorithms are bifurcated based on the scale of the feature
selection datasets into categories targeting conventional and high-
dimensional datasets. For conventional-sized feature selection tasks,
one line of research focuses on enhancing the feature search capabilities
of the WOA [17,31–34], such as the multi-strategy multi-objective WOA
proposed by Yang et al., which boosts the feature selection performance
of the algorithm [32]. Another line of research combines WOA-based
wrappers and filters to accomplish feature selection, such as Yab
et al., who enhanced feature selection capabilities by integrating
modified WOA with filters [35]. In the context of high-dimensional
feature selection challenges, one approach involves augmenting
feature recognition capabilities in vast spaces by modifying or amal-
gamating other algorithms to forge new search strategies. For example,
Too et al. restructured the WOA with a spatial boundary strategy,
facilitating the identification of latent features in expansive feature
spaces [36]. As research progresses, the potency of multi-population
structures in complex solution spaces has been recognized, leading to
the proposal of multi-population WOA algorithms for intricate feature
selection tasks [35–38]. Sun et al., for example, introduced a two-stage
adaptive fuzzy neighborhood feature selection algorithm based on
WOA, utilizing an adaptive neighborhood search radius to segregate
elite and ordinary populations, thereby effectively executing the feature
selection process [38]. However, the method has a small number of
subpopulations and a fixed number of subpopulations, all of which limit
the ability to mine for global information. Current studies utilizing
multi-population WOA algorithms for feature selection are scant and
warrant further investigation.
Moreover, in recent years, EC feature selection algorithms integrated
with attention mechanisms have been proposed. For example, Xue et al.
[40] developed a large-scale feature selection ranking algorithm based
on an external attention mechanism, which uses an attention module
trained during the process to rank features by importance and accom-
plish feature selection through EC methods. This approach performs
well in large-scale feature selection tasks, but it is sensitive to hyper-
parameters [40].
2.3.2. NON-EC-based feature selection
In recent years, scholars have researched feature selection algo-
rithms for NON-EC, such as ReliefF [41] and mutual information [42].
Zhou et al. [43] proposed a hybrid feature selection method that uses the
absolute values of correlation coefficients between features as weights
for redundancy in mutual information representation, achieving feature
selection. Dai et al. [44] introduced a fuzzy combined entropy feature
selection algorithm that simultaneously considers global and local
feature relevancies, leveraging both global and local information.

However, these methods may still fail to remove complex redundant
features because of their inability to communicate directly with classi-
fication outcomes. Furthermore, researchers have explored the use of
linear dimensionality reduction techniques for feature selection, such as
singular value decomposition (SVD) [45] and linear discriminant anal-
ysis (LDA) [45] . These linear techniques have shown certain effective-
ness, yet they have limitations, such as computational difficulties with
SVD and poor performance of LDA on non-Gaussian and small-sample
datasets. Some scholars have also experimented with nonlinear dimen-
sionality reduction techniques to select and filter high-dimensional
features, such as kernel principal component analysis (KPCA) [46] and
multidimensional scaling (MDS) [46] . However, the performance of
KPCA can vary significantly depending on the chosen kernel, whereas
MDS performance is highly susceptible to outliers in the data.
With the advancement of deep learning, feature selection for deep
features has increasingly gained attention. Some researchers have inte-
grated classical feature selection algorithms into deep neural networks
to filter deep features. Thakkar et al. [47] proposed a deep neural
network feature selection method based on the fusion of statistical
importance, which compresses and filters features based on the rank
derived from the statistical importance of variance, mean, and median
differences of deep features. Acikgoz et al. [47] combined the ReliefF
method with the feature extraction layer of convolutional neural net-
works, facilitating the identification and selection of significant features
in the subset. Furthermore, learning-based feature selection methods
represent another major category of deep feature selection approaches
[48] . Channel selection in deep neural networks can be considered a
special task of feature selection. Xue et al. [48] introduced an
attention-based progressive channel selection in the task of differen-
tiable neural architecture search, effectively identifying key channels.
To further increase the accuracy of channel identification, Xue et al. [49]
improved the attention mechanism and proposed a dual-attention
adaptive weight architecture, which significantly enhanced the recog-
nition of important channels. Despite the promising results of feature
selection methods for deep neural networks, their high demand for
computational resources may limit the applicability of these approaches
in certain scenarios.
In summary, EC-based feature selection algorithms hold significant
potential in addressing high-dimensional feature selection challenges.
WOA, as one of the recent representative algorithms in the EC domain,
has also received considerable attention in the field of feature selection.
However, current studies predominantly focus on single-swarm strate-
gies, which may limit performance in the face of complex feature spaces
presented by high-dimensional datasets [50] . Although some studies
have recognized the advantages of multi-swarm algorithms in address-
ing complex optimization problems, introducing multi-population
structures to high-dimensional feature selection challenges, the fixed
number of sub-populations in existing multi-swarm structures hinders
synergistic interactions among individuals, necessitating deeper
research into multi-swarm construction mechanisms. Moreover, the
feature space transformation function in the existing high-dimensional
feature selection algorithms mostly adopts random thresholds, which
has the problem of invalid probability flipping, which may lead to the
loss of effective features. To overcome these challenges, we propose the
EMSWOA algorithm, which, by designing a dynamic multi-swarm
mechanism and elite tuning strategy, aims to enhance the feature se-
lection capabilities in high-dimensional feature selection problems, of-
fering a new and effective solution in this field.
3. The proposed method
To improve the quality of feature selection, we propose an EMSWOA.
Specifically, the general framework of the EMSWOA is presented in 3.1.
The dynamic multi-swarm mechanism based on the centroid distance is
described in detail in subsection 3.2. In 3.3, the elite tuning strategy is
described, and the fitness function is described in 3.4.

Second, the subpopulations of the EMSWOA are dynamically con-
structed through the proposed dynamic multi-swarm mechanism, which
is based on the centroid distance. The topology between subpopulations
of dynamic multi-swarm is dynamic and reorganized. Compared with
the static topology, the dynamic topological multiple population struc-

space more diverse and convenient, which facilitates the exchange of
merit-seeking information among subpopulations through population
reorganization, thus enhancing the diversity of populations in the al-
gorithm space, slowing down convergence and avoiding premature
convergence.
Based on the dynamic multiple populations constructed, the in-

3.2. Dynamic multi-swarm mechanism based on centroid distance
The global search performance of metaheuristic algorithms is influ-
enced by their local exploitation and global exploration abilities. In
multiple swarm evolutionary algorithms, local information exploitation
is achieved through intra-subpopulation search, and global information
exploration is achieved through interactive recombination between
subpopulations. However, when performing a search within a subpop-
ulation, the size of the subpopulation ’ s search space and the number of
individuals within it have a significantly influence over the information
exploited in that space; specifically, the larger the search space is, the

3.3. Elite tuning strategy
Inspired by the literature [3] , this paper employs the commonly used
S-type function in this field as the transfer function to achieve conver-

4.1. Experimental setup
4.1.1. High-dimensional datasets
To validate the performance and generalization ability of the EMS-
WOA on different datasets, a total of 19 benchmark datasets [24,54]
commonly used in high-dimensional feature selection performance
evaluation were selected. These benchmark datasets have different
categories and sample sizes, with unbalanced data between categories.
Complex and diverse categories and sample distributions can more
effectively evaluate the generalization capability of FS algorithms under
complex distribution conditions and have the ability to identify effective
feature subsets. This paper draws on a commonly used selection of
datasets from extensive literature [24,54] in this field, selecting 19
benchmark datasets composed of one-dimensional feature data of type
1 × n , where n denotes the number of original features, as detailed in
Table 2 .
4.1.2. Comparison algorithms
We select two classes of comparison algorithms for analysis with the
EMSWOA algorithm. The first class is the evolutionary computation
class of algorithms of the same type as the WOA, which includes: two
classical algorithms of the evolutionary computation class and five state-
of-the-art (SOTA) approaches in the field of high-dimensional FS. The
latest algorithm is the GOOSE algorithm (GOOSE) [55] . Five SOTA
methods are used in the field of high-dimensional FS, including the
competitive swarm optimizer feature selection algorithm (CSOFS) [56] ,
the binary chaotic crow search algorithm (BCCAS) [57] , the heteroge-
neous composite learning and dynamic multi-swarm particle swarm
algorithm (HCLDMS-PSO) [58] , the modified whale optimization algo-
rithm (MWOA) [59] , and the hybrid whale optimization algorithm
(HWOAG) [16] . The relevant parameter settings are shown in Table 3 .
Another class of representative FS algorithms of the non-
evolutionary computation class includes discriminative FS based on
structured sparse subspace learning modules (S
2
DFS) [60] , FS based on
adaptive graph learning and constraints (EGCFS) [61] , multi-criteria
decision-based multi-label feature selection (MFS-MCDM) [62] , and
support vector machines with recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE)
[63] and its equivalent extended algorithm (MSVM-RFE) [64] and
RFE-CBR [65] with correlated bias reduction and the Online group
streaming feature selection based on fuzzy neighbourhood granular ball
rough sets (OGSFS-FNGBRS) [66] .
4.2. Experimental analysis
4.2.1. Comparison of average accuracy rates
The EMSWOA was tested with seven comparison algorithms on 19
benchmark datasets with high-dimensional feature selection. Table 4
presents the results of the experiments for average classification accu-
racy and standard deviation (STD), with the optimal values in bold.
First, comparing the EMSWOA algorithm to the 6 comparison algo-
rithms overall, the EMSWOA achieved the best average classification
accuracy on all 19 high-dimensional datasets. Among them, the average
classification accuracy of the EMSWOA reached 100 % on the DLBCL,
Leukemia_3, ALLAML and Leukemia datasets, and only the EMSWOA
algorithm reached the theoretical optimum on these four datasets.
Moreover, the STD of the EMSWOA algorithm is 0 on the DLBCL, Leu-
kemia_3, ALLAML and Leukemia datasets, which indicates that the
improved the EMSWOA not only has a higher accuracy in finding the
best, but also the robustness of the algorithm. Secondly, compared to the
best of four excellent algorithms in high-dimensional FS, the EMSWOA
algorithm improves accuracy by more than 3 % on average over nine
datasets, indicating that the EMSWOA is still more competitive even
when compared to new high-dimensional FS approaches that have been
proposed recently. Compared with the HCLDMS-PSO algorithm, which
is also a multi-swarm algorithm, the EMSWOA algorithm has an average
accuracy improvement of more than 6 % on eleven datasets, which in-
dicates that the dynamic multi-population through the central distance
can effectively improve the algorithm ’ s mining ability, and is more
conducive to learning for local information, which enhances the algo-
rithm ’ s exploitation ability. The EMSWOA algorithm obtained the
highest classification accuracy on all 19 datasets, but only obtained a
smaller standard deviation on 5 datasets, which may be due to the fact
that the elite tuning process is affected by the current optimal solution in
the iteration cycle, which increases the volatility of the EMSWOA while
speeding up the convergence. Overall, the EMSWOA algorithm searches
for a better subset of features and obtains higher classification accuracy.
We present a plot of the average accuracy convergence curves for the
EMSWOA and the comparison algorithms on the 19 datasets, as shown
in Fig. 7 . Overall, the EMSWOA outperforms all the comparative algo-
rithms in terms of convergence curves on 19 datasets, and the algorithm
converges faster and with less fluctuation in the convergence curves,
indicating that the EMSWOA can perform a faster search for the best
solution on the high-dimensional feature selection dataset. In addition,
the EMSWOA converges slightly slower than the comparison algorithms
do in the early iterations on the Leukemia_3, GLI_85, SMK-CAN-187,
ALLAML, Colon, Lymphoma, Nci9, Leukemia, Prostage-GE and Yale
datasets, but it is superior to the comparison algorithms in terms of
convergence accuracy. The reason for the above phenomenon is that the
multi-swarm division in the EMSWOA algorithm initially limits the
exploration space, thus restricting global search capabilities. However,
the dynamic multi-swarm mechanism of the EMSWOA prevents the al-
gorithm from being trapped in local optima because it has a single
population. Therefore, with increasing iteration numbers, the EMSWOA
demonstrates stronger global optimization capabilities.

and dynamic multi-swarm mechanism are reliable. Additionally, the
EMSWOA exhibited superior performance in reducing the average

ities. Specifically, the elite tuning strategy exhibits a strong capacity for
effective feature elimination. Concurrently, the dynamic multi-swarm
mechanism improves the quality of the best individuals during the
elite correction phase by fostering greater diversity within the popula-
tion, which in turn enhances the quality of elite correction. These two
components work in synergy to significantly enhance the feature se-
lection capabilities of the EMSWOA.
4.2.6. Comparison with the latest EA-based feature selection algorithms
To further validate the efficacy of the EMSWOA algorithm in high-
dimensional feature selection problems, we selected seven of the most
recently published algorithms that have demonstrated exceptional per-
formance in this area. Among them, the evolutionary dual-task feature
selection method based on probability-based initialization particle
swarm optimization (PPSO-EDT) [25] , the objective space
constraint-based evolutionary algorithm (OSC-EA) [26] , the copula
entropy-based binary enhanced golden jackal optimization (CE- BEGJO)
[27] , the a decomposition-based multi-population evolutionary algo-
rithm for high-dimensional feature selection (MPEA-FS) [30] and the
binary variant of the Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves algorithm (BAFT)
based on adaptive elite opposition-based AFT (BAEOAFT) [29] were
published in 2024 and represent the most recent advances, while the
modified gray wolf optimization with copula entropy filter methods
(MGWO-C) [28] is a representative algorithm published in 2023. A
comparative analysis of these seven state-of-the-art algorithms against
EMSWOA is presented in Table 9 . Furthermore, the average classifica-
tion accuracy (ACC) and the average number of selected features (NUM)
cited in Table 8 are sourced from publicly available literature, where a
’ - ’ denotes that the corresponding data were not provided in the
publications.
From the comparative results in Table 9 , it is evident that the feature
subsets selected by the EMSWOA generally achieved higher classifica-
tion accuracies. Specifically, the EMSWOA attained the highest classi-
fication accuracy in 16 out of 19 datasets, with the most significant
improvement observed in the Yale dataset, where the average accuracy
increased by 13.51 %. Moreover, only the EMSWOA algorithm achieved
a 100 % accuracy rate in the DLBCL, Leukemia_3, ALLAML, and Leu-
kemia datasets. The EMSWOA slightly underperformed compared to the
optimal values in the Leukemia_1, GLI_85, and Nci9 datasets. Analyzing
from the perspective of the average number of features, the EMSWOA
achieved the highest classification accuracies with the fewest features in
the Lymphoma, Prostate-GE, and Yale datasets. In other datasets, the
number of features selected by the EMSWOA was higher than the min-
imal feature count of the comparative methods; however, excessive
filtering of features can lead to the loss of discriminative attributes. The
comparative methods tend to overly compress the feature set, dimin-
ishing their focus on improving accuracy and consequently losing
valuable features. For instance, in the Brain_Tumor2 dataset, the optimal
feature subset selected by the MPEA-FS contained an average of only
5.63 features, yet the classification accuracy was only 78.57 %, signifi-
cantly lower than the 92.40 % achieved by the EMSWOA. Thanks to its
elite tuning strategy, the EMSWOA effectively relearns and corrects for
missing effective features, thus favoring the identification and retention
of discriminative features while eliminating redundant and irrelevant
ones. Therefore, compared to the latest state-of-the-art methods, the
EMSWOA algorithm still exhibits strong competitiveness in high-
dimensional feature selection tasks.

4.2.8. Discussion
The EMSWOA method proposed in this paper enhances the ability of
the algorithm to jump out of local optimal solutions through the dy-
namic multi-swarm mechanism based on centroid distance, and through
the mining and detection of local information by subpopulations. In
addition, the quality of the candidate solutions is improved by the
proposed elite tuning strategy, which corrects for the invalid probability
flips during discrete space mapping, thus accelerating convergence. The
experimental results also show that the EMSWOA algorithm can achieve
higher classification accuracy with fewer features than the comparison
methods can achieve. The EMSWOA feature selection algorithm has
broader application value and can be utilized to address high-
dimensional data reduction issues in the field of bioinformatics pro-
cessing [3]. It can also be applied in contexts such as IoT intrusion
detection [68], thereby introducing new technological options for the
field of data statistics and analysis.
However, the EMSWOA also has some limitations. First, the EMS-
WOA is only adapted to the case of a fixed length of basic features, and is
not capable of coping with complex environments where the length of
features changes, and can be researched in the later stage for the
problem of variable-length feature selection. In addition, the EMSWOA
only performs feature selection for single-label data, and cannot be used
for multi-label task, and the EMSWOA algorithm can be extended to
multi-label feature selection tasks in the future.
5. Conclusions and future work
In this paper, a dynamic multi-swarm WOA based on elite tuning is
proposed to solve the high-dimensional feature selection problem. In the
EMSWOA, dynamic multiple populations are first constructed by
designing a centroid distance metric based approach to improve the
exploitation ability of the EMSWOA in the decision space while main-
taining population diversity. In addition, to correct for the invalid
probability flip-flop problem when converting to discrete space, we
design an elite tuning strategy to improve the interference of dynamic
thresholding on the feature importance measure, enhance the quality of
the subset of candidate features, and improve the performance of the
EMSWOA algorithm. Based on a dynamic multi-swarm mechanism and
an elite tuning strategy, the experimental results of the EMSWOA on 12
real-world, high-dimensional datasets show that, compared with other
superior algorithms, the EMSWOA can remove redundant and irrelevant
features more effectively and improve the accuracy of classification.
The dynamic multi-swarm mechanism based on the centroid dis-
tance and elite tuning strategy in EMSWOA proposed in this paper can
be used not only to improve the WOA algorithm but also to improve
swarm intelligence algorithms. The newly proposed strategy enhances
the optimization capabilities of algorithms, aiding in providing better
search methodologies for the fields of machine learning and data sci-
ence. For example, the improved swarm intelligence algorithms can be
employed for optimizing neural network parameters, as well as for
searching neural network architectures and processing and analyzing
EEG data, among other tasks, thereby offering novel solutions for these
domains.
Future work could improve the global optimization process of the
WOA by combining relevant algorithmic ideas from machine learning
and deep learning, such as transfer learning, to accelerate the conver-
gence of the algorithm. In addition, the scope of application of algo-
rithms can also be expanded through domain-specific research. For
example, the scope of application of algorithms can be explored for areas
such as affective computing, and a priori knowledge-guided evolu-
tionary updating strategies can be designed for specific domains to
improve feature selection performance in the target domain.
