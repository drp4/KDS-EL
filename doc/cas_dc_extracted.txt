ABSTRACT
High-dimensionalmicroarraydata,characterizedbythousandsoffeaturesbutonlytensorhundredsof
samples, impose severe computational and generalization burdens on classification models. Feature
selection has become an essential preprocessing step to build accurate and interpretable classifiers
from such data. Among metaheuristic approaches, the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) has shown
promiseforwrapper-basedfeatureselection,yetexistingbinarySSAvariantsrelyontransferfunctions
that create an inherent probability bottleneck near 0.5, making it difficult to decisively exclude
irrelevant features from high-dimensional spaces. This paper proposes an Elite-Guided Dynamic
Feature Probability Sparrow Search Algorithm (EDP-SSA) that replaces the conventional transfer-
function binarization with a dynamic feature probability (DFP) vector directly updated through
Bernoulli sampling. The DFP vector is initialized at a low value (0.1) and adjusted at each iteration
based on the observed selection frequency across the population, implementing a frequency-driven
elimination mechanism. An elite consensus tuning (ECT) strategy with delayed activation maintains
a sliding window of the best binary masks from recent generations and applies majority voting to
reinforce features consistently selected by high-quality solutions. A time-varying adaptive producer
ratio increases linearly from 15% to 35% during evolution to balance exploration and exploitation,
and a greedy single-feature flipping local search refines the final subset under a bounded evaluation
budget. Experiments on 16 high-dimensional microarray datasets (2,000–22,283 features) against 10
competingalgorithmsdemonstratethatEDP-SSAachievesthehighestaverageclassificationaccuracy
of 91.88% while selecting only 1.04% of features on average, compared with 89.27% accuracy and
7.98% features for the best competitor (bIRIME). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests at the 0.05 significance
level confirm statistically significant improvements in 140 of 160 pairwise comparisons, and the
Friedman test ranks EDP-SSA first with an average rank of 1.125. Ablation experiments quantify the
progressive contribution of each component: the DFP mechanism raises the average accuracy from
85.30% (basic SSA) to 88.03%, the ECT provides an additional improvement to 89.19%, and the full
model reaches 91.88%.

2. Related Work
2.1. Feature Selection Methods
Featureselectionaimstoidentifyacompactanddiscrim-
inativesubsetoffeaturesfromtheoriginalhigh-dimensional
space. Classical filter methods, such as mutual information
(MI)[30],chi-squaretest[23],andRelief[21],evaluateeach
feature independently based on its statistical relationship
with the class labels. While computationally efficient, filter

methods generally ignore feature–feature interactions and
may fail to identify complementary feature combinations
that are jointly informative but individually weak.
Wrapper methods address this limitation by employing
a learning algorithm to evaluate candidate feature subsets.
Common search strategies include forward selection, back-
ward elimination, and recursive feature elimination (RFE)
[22]. Support vector machine recursive feature elimination
(SVM-RFE) [16] has been widely used in bioinformatics
for gene selection from microarray data. Although wrap-
permethodstypicallyachievehigherclassificationaccuracy
thanfiltermethods,theircomputationalcostgrowsexponen-
tiallywiththenumberoffeatures,makingthemlesspractical
forveryhigh-dimensionalproblemswithoutefficientsearch
strategies.
Embedded methods, such as LASSO [38], elastic net
[43], and tree-based feature importance [4], perform feature
selection as part of the model training process. These meth-
odsofferagoodbalancebetweenefficiencyandaccuracybut
may be limited by the specific model assumptions and may
not generalize well across different classification tasks.
2.2. Swarm Intelligence for Feature Selection
In recent years, swarm intelligence algorithms have
emerged as a popular choice for wrapper-based feature
selection due to their ability to explore large combinatorial
search spaces efficiently. Genetic algorithms (GA) [17]
use selection, crossover, and mutation operators to evolve
a population of feature subsets. Binary particle swarm
optimization (BPSO) [19] adapts the continuous PSO
frameworktobinaryspacesusingtransferfunctions.Binary
grey wolf optimizer (BGWO) [8] and binary ant colony
optimization (ACO) [7] have also been applied to feature
selection with promising results.
The sparrow search algorithm (SSA), proposed by Xue
and Shen [42], is a relatively recent swarm intelligence
method inspired by the foraging and anti-predation behav-
iors of sparrows. The algorithm divides the population into
producers, which are responsible for exploring new food
sources, and scroungers, which follow the producers to ob-
tain food. A fraction of sparrows are also assigned as scouts
that perform anti-predation behaviors. SSA has attracted
attention in feature selection due to its fast convergence,
simple structure, and few control parameters.
Several binary variants of SSA have been developed
for feature selection. Gad et al. [11] proposed an improved
binary SSA (iBSSA) for feature selection in data classifi-
cation. Liang et al. [24] introduced an enhanced sparrow
search swarm optimizer (ESSSO) that integrates multiple
searchstrategiesforhigh-dimensionalcontinuousoptimiza-
tion problems. Tang et al. [37] proposed a chaotic SSA
(CSSA) that introduces chaotic maps to enhance population
diversity and escape local optima. Despite these improve-
ments, binary SSA variants for feature selection share the
samefundamentallimitation:theyrelyontransferfunctions
to convert continuous positions into binary decisions.
2.3. Limitations of Existing Approaches
The most prevalent approach for binarizing continu-
ous swarm intelligence algorithms is the use of transfer
functions.Sigmoidfunctions[19],V-shapedfunctions[26],
and U-shaped functions [28] have been proposed to map
continuous values to selection probabilities. However, these
functions impose an inherent constraint: when a continuous
position is near zero, the selection probability approaches
0.5,meaningeachfeaturehasroughlyequalchancesofbeing
selected or excluded. In high-dimensional feature selection,
where the goal is to identify a small discriminative subset
from thousands of candidates, this 0.5 bottleneck severely
limits the algorithm’s ability to eliminate irrelevant features
effectively.
To address this issue, several alternative strategies have
been explored. Sun et al. [36] developed a binary SSA
(BSSFS) for feature selection, yet it still relies on transfer
functions for binarization and thus inherits the probability
bottleneck.Huangetal.[18]introducedbIRIMEtomitigate
the exploitation–exploration imbalance of the RIME opti-
mizer for feature selection, but its binarization is likewise
transfer-function based. Shikoun et al. [32] proposed a bi-
nary crayfish optimization algorithm (BinCOA) for feature
selection, and Beheshti [3] combined time-varying sine and
V-shaped transfer functions in a binary marine predators
algorithm; both still convert continuous positions through
fixed mappings and cannot decisively eliminate irrelevant
features.
Another limitation of existing SSA variants is the fixed
producer–scrounger ratio. In standard SSA, approximately
20%ofthepopulationservesasproducersthroughouttheen-
tireevolutionaryprocess.Thisstaticallocationcannotadapt
to different search stages: early iterations may benefit from
more producers for broader exploration, while later itera-
tionsmayrequiremorescroungersforfine-grainedexploita-
tion. Several adaptive strategies have been proposed for
relatedalgorithms.Mengetal.[25]proposedthebirdswarm
algorithm, which employs adaptive control of flight behav-
iors to balance exploration and exploitation. Mirjalili et al.
[27] proposed the grey wolf optimizer, whose exploration–
exploitationbalanceiscontrolledbyaparameterthatdecays
linearly over iterations. Wang et al. [39] combined multiple
search strategies in a multi-strategy SSA variant, demon-
stratingthathybridapproachescanimproveperformanceon
complex optimization problems.
These observations motivate the design of the proposed
EDP-SSA, which addresses all three limitations simultane-
ously through a dynamic feature probability mechanism, an
elite consensus tuning strategy, and a time-varying adaptive
producer ratio.

3.7. Local Search Post-processing
Aftertheevolutionaryprocessterminates,agreedylocal
searchprocedureisappliedtothebestbinarymasktofurther
refine the selected feature subset. The local search operates
by iteratively flipping a single feature (changing𝑥𝑗 from 0
to 1 or from 1 to 0) and evaluating the resulting fitness.
At each step, a random permutation of all𝐷features
is generated, and each feature is flipped one at a time. If
flippingfeature𝑗improvesthefitness,thechangeisaccepted
immediately and the procedure restarts from a new random
permutation. If no improvement is found after a complete
passthroughallfeatures,thelocalsearchterminates.Topre-
vent excessive computation on high-dimensional datasets,
the total number of fitness evaluations during local search
is bounded by𝐸max = 5000. The procedure is summarized
as follows:
1. Initialize𝐱 ∗ ←𝐱 best,𝑓 ∗ ←Fitness(𝐱 ∗), count←1.
2. Generate a random permutation𝜋of{1,2,…,𝐷}.
3. For each𝑗∈𝜋: flip𝑥 ∗
𝑗, evaluate fitness, if improved
accept and go to Step 2; otherwise revert.
4. If count<𝐸 max, go to Step 2; otherwise return𝐱∗.
This single-feature flipping neighborhood is computation-
ally efficient and has been shown to be effective for binary
feature selection problems [22].
It is important to clarify the evaluation protocol behind
the local search to rule out overfitting concerns. The local
searchoptimizesexactlythesamefitnessfunctionastheevo-
lutionarysearchphase,i.e.,5-foldstratifiedcross-validation
with a fixed fold partition (a fixed random state), so it never
observes the final evaluation folds. The final reported ac-
curacy of every algorithm—including EDP-SSA after local
search—is always recomputed with an independent 10-fold
stratified cross-validation. The bounded evaluation budget
(𝐸max = 5000) further limits the degree to which the
local search can adapt to the search-phase partitions. In the
ablationstudy,variant(iii)(SSA+DFP+Elite)excludesboth
theadaptiveproducerratioandthelocalsearch,soitsgapof
2.69percentage points to the full EDP-SSA quantifies the
combined contribution of these two components. To isolate
thecontributionofthelocalsearchalone,weadditionallyran
thefullmodelwiththelocal-searchpost-processingdisabled
on three representative datasets (colon, SRBCT, and lung)
under the same 10 random seeds. The local search adds
an average of1.45percentage points of accuracy on these
datasets (from94.01%to95.46%), with the largest gain of
2.82percentage points on SRBCT and the smallest of0.39
percentage points on lung, which confirms that the fourth
component contributes measurably to the final performance
withoutinflatingthereportedaccuracybeyonditslegitimate
improvement.

4.4. Statistical Significance Analysis
To rigorously assess whether the performance
improvements of EDP-SSA over competing algorithms

rank test [40] is applied at the 0.05 significance level. For
each dataset, the 10 accuracy values of EDP-SSA (one
per independent run) and the 10 accuracy values of each
competing algorithm form two paired samples of size 10,
and the test is performed on these paired vectors rather than
on the aggregate means. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is
a non-parametric statistical test suitable for comparing two
related samples, and it does not require the assumption of
normal distribution. The null hypothesis is that there is no
significant difference between the two algorithms.

5. Conclusion and Future Work
This paper proposed an Elite-Guided Dynamic Feature
ProbabilitySparrowSearchAlgorithm(EDP-SSA)forhigh-
dimensional feature selection. The algorithm addresses
three fundamental limitations of existing binary SSA
variants through four complementary mechanisms. First,
the dynamic feature probability (DFP) mechanism replaces
conventional transfer functions with a directly evolving
probability vector, enabling Bernoulli sampling to generate
binary masks without the 0.5 probability bottleneck.
Second, the elite consensus tuning (ECT) strategy with
delayed activation exploits historical best solutions through
majority voting, providing robust probability correction
that reinforces consistently selected features and suppresses
unanimously excluded ones. Third, a time-varying adaptive
producer ratio increases linearly from 15% to 35% during
evolution, enabling a smooth transition from exploration
to exploitation. Fourth, a greedy single-feature flipping
local search refines the final feature subset under a bounded
evaluation budget. By shifting the binarization paradigm
from position-dependent transfer functions to evidence-
driven probability evolution, EDP-SSA offers a new
perspective for high-dimensional discrete optimization
beyond conventional swarm intelligence.
Experiments on the 16 microarray benchmarks against
10 competing algorithms show the effectiveness of the
proposed approach. EDP-SSA achieved the highest average
classification accuracy of 91.88%, outperforming the
second-best method bIRIME (89.27%) by 2.61 percentage
points. More importantly, EDP-SSA selected only 1.04%
of features on average, compared to 7.98% for the nearest
competitor, confirming its superior feature compression
capability. The algorithm also obtained the best average
