High-dimensional data have become a common challenge in classification tasks across genomics, text mining, and computer vision. Datasets in these domains often contain tens of thousands of features while providing only tens or hundreds of labeled samples, a regime denoted *p >> n*. Under such conditions, standard classifiers are prone to overfitting, computational overhead grows rapidly, and model interpretability degrades, which are typical consequences of the curse of dimensionality (Li et al., 2017; Guyon and Elisseeff, 2003). Feature selection (FS) mitigates these effects by identifying a compact subset of informative features before model training. However, the space of candidate feature subsets expands exponentially with the number of dimensions *D*, making FS an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem (Liang et al., 2025); in addition, marginal feature scores cannot fully capture the feature interactions that determine classifier performance.

FS methods are commonly categorized into filter, wrapper, and embedded approaches (Kohavi and John, 1997; Li et al., 2017). Filter methods evaluate features with statistical measures and are computationally efficient, but they often ignore feature interactions and classifier-specific relevance. Embedded methods integrate selection into model training and offer a balance between efficiency and accuracy, while remaining tied to the assumptions of the underlying model. Wrapper methods train a classifier on each candidate subset and therefore tend to produce higher accuracy, yet their search cost grows rapidly with dimensionality. To search the large, non-convex solution space of FS, evolutionary computation has been widely adopted (Kennedy and Eberhart, 1995; Shi and Eberhart, 1998; Kennedy and Eberhart, 1997). Population-based stochastic search can explore candidate feature subsets without gradient information or restrictive model assumptions, and evolutionary algorithms such as particle swarm optimization (PSO), genetic algorithms, and differential evolution have shown strong performance on high-dimensional FS benchmarks.

PSO is among the most widely used evolutionary algorithms for FS because of its compact representation, simple update rules, and fast convergence. Nevertheless, standard PSO-based FS methods face three structural limitations when applied to sparse, high-dimensional search spaces. First, random initialization scatters the initial swarm across the entire binary hypercube, while an effective feature subset usually activates only a small fraction of the available dimensions; a substantial portion of the limited evaluation budget is therefore spent on dense, low-quality solutions before search can concentrate on sparse regions. Second, conventional uniform bit-flip mutation treats every feature identically and discards the per-feature improvement information accumulated during previous evaluations, so the search cannot progressively exploit historically effective dimensions. Third, most PSO-based FS methods combine classification error and feature ratio using a fixed weight vector throughout the entire search, although feature reduction and accuracy refinement have different temporal requirements: redundant features should be removed aggressively at the beginning, while accuracy should be refined on a compact subset at the later stage.

Recent evolutionary FS studies have attempted to mitigate these issues through improved initialization, adaptive mutation, or modified fitness formulations (Wang and Jia, 2022; Wang et al., 2022; Xue and Zhang, 2024; Wei et al., 2025). These improvements are valuable, but they generally target individual components and do not jointly address cold-start inefficiency, knowledge accumulation, and rigid objective weighting. Multi-task PSO has recently shown particular promise for fixed-length, high-dimensional FS: for example, MEL (Wang et al., 2024) uses fixed-length binary encoding and cooperative subpopulations to scale to tens of thousands of features and demonstrates strong efficiency. However, existing multi-task PSO frameworks still face unresolved challenges: population construction usually provides little prior guidance in sparse spaces, feature-importance evidence obtained during search is not systematically accumulated, and objective preferences remain fixed across evolutionary phases.

To address these limitations, this paper proposes KDS-EL (Knowledge-Guided Dual-Phase Swarm Evolutionary Learning), a closed-loop knowledge-guided evolutionary framework that integrates three complementary mechanisms into the multi-task PSO architecture. Knowledge is progressively introduced, accumulated, and reused throughout the evolutionary process. First, mutual-information-guided initialization with adaptive re-initialization injects prior feature-relevance knowledge into the initial population and restarts stagnated individuals, reducing cold-start waste. Second, the Knowledge-guided Elite Refinement (KER) strategy accumulates a cross-iteration feature-importance matrix from fitness comparisons already performed during search, applies exponential decay to discount obsolete evidence, and steers elite solutions toward historically effective features within a bounded refinement budget. Third, Two-Phase Weight Scheduling switches the dual-objective weights from aggressive feature reduction in the early search to fine-grained accuracy refinement in the later search, aligning the optimization preference with the temporal structure of FS. The three mechanisms operate at complementary levels of the search process and are independently verifiable through component contribution analysis.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1. **MI-guided initialization with adaptive re-initialization.** We inject feature-relevance knowledge into population construction and restore diversity when stagnation occurs.
2. **Knowledge-guided Elite Refinement (KER).** We accumulate cross-iteration feature-importance evidence from fitness comparisons already performed during search, discount obsolete evidence by exponential decay, and perform knowledge-biased elite perturbation with 90% guided and 10% random flips within a bounded refinement budget.
3. **Two-Phase Weight Scheduling.** We replace static dual-objective weights with an early feature-reduction phase and a late accuracy-refinement phase, aligning optimization preferences with the temporal structure of FS.
4. **Systematic validation.** We conduct experiments on 20 high-dimensional benchmarks spanning 500 to 54,675 features and 14 fixed-length methods, including component contribution analysis and parameter sensitivity analysis.

Extensive experiments show that KDS-EL ranks first with a mean accuracy of 0.9574 and 87.8 selected features on average, outperforming the strongest single-objective and multi-objective competitors by 3.97 and 4.42 percentage points, respectively, while achieving a 3.4x speedup over MEL (51.4 s vs. 175.0 s); Wilcoxon and Holm-Bonferroni tests confirm statistically significant improvements over all 13 competitors.
