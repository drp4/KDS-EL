% --------------------------- 1. Introduction ---------------------------
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

High-dimensional data present fundamental challenges to pattern classification across diverse domains, including bioinformatics, transcriptomics, and biomedical diagnostics. These domains frequently involve datasets containing thousands or tens of thousands of continuous features alongside severely restricted sample sizes, creating ultra-high-dimensional and sample-scarce regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed available observational samples $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. Under such adverse conditions, conventional machine learning algorithms suffer from severe overfitting, prohibitive computational costs, and degraded model interpretability due to the curse of dimensionality~\cite{lili2017,guyon2003}. Feature selection has consequently emerged as an indispensable preprocessing technique aimed at identifying a minimal, highly discriminative subset of relevant features while discarding redundant and uninformative dimensions. Nevertheless, discovering an optimal feature subset is an inherently combinatorial problem whose discrete search space scales exponentially as $2^D$, rendering exhaustive evaluation NP-hard~\cite{liang2025}. Furthermore, univariate feature ranking criteria evaluate dimensions in isolation, fundamentally failing to capture multi-feature synergy and non-linear interactions essential for accurate classification.

Feature selection methods are broadly categorized into filter, wrapper, and embedded paradigms~\cite{kohavi1997,lili2017}. Filter methods evaluate candidate features based on intrinsic statistical or information-theoretic metrics independently of inductive classifiers, offering high computational speed but frequently overlooking complex multi-feature synergy. Embedded methods incorporate feature selection directly into learning model parameter estimation, providing intermediate computational efficiency; however, their selection performance remains architecturally tied to specific classifiers. Conversely, wrapper methods directly guide feature subset exploration using classifier predictive feedback, naturally capturing non-linear feature interactions and yielding superior classification accuracy, albeit at the expense of intensive computational evaluation. To navigate the combinatorial discrete search space of wrapper feature selection, evolutionary computation has been extensively investigated as a powerful population-based optimization paradigm~\cite{xue2016survey,liang2025}. By maintaining a diverse population of candidate solutions, evolutionary algorithms explore the discrete combinatorial space without requiring gradient information or restrictive convexity assumptions. Among various evolutionary meta-heuristics, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) has demonstrated prominent utility in feature selection tasks due to its conceptual simplicity, rapid information-sharing dynamics, and flexible velocity-position representation~\cite{kennedy1995,shi1998,kennedy1997}.

Despite its widespread adoption, canonical PSO encounters three critical structural bottlenecks when navigating ultra-high-dimensional and sample-scarce search spaces. First, standard uniform random initialization scatters particles indiscriminately across the candidate space, whereas optimal feature subsets occupy an ultra-sparse combinatorial subspace with cardinality $d^{\star} \ll D$. Consequently, a substantial volume of expensive wrapper evaluations is squandered on dense, uninformative candidate masks, severely exacerbating early evaluation latency and sample-scarce overfitting. Second, conventional uniform variation heuristics treat all candidate dimensions equally and operate memorylessly across generations; by discarding the historical feature-level fitness feedback accumulated during previous iterations, the swarm fails to exploit verified discriminative dimensions and degenerates into stochastic random drift. Third, existing evolutionary wrappers enforce static, fixed objective weights to balance classification accuracy against subset cardinality throughout evolution. This static formulation creates a fundamental evolutionary phase misalignment: the search inherently demands aggressive dimensional pruning during early exploration to substantially contract vast noise subspaces, whereas fine-grained decision-boundary tuning is essential during late exploitation to maximize inductive classification accuracy.

Recent evolutionary feature selection studies have investigated specialized strategies to mitigate high-dimensional search degradation, including feature-weight velocity steering in FWPSO~\cite{wangjia2022}, adaptive differential evolution weighting in SaWDE~\cite{wang2022}, surrogate-guided perturbation in IGPSO~\cite{xuezhang2024}, and multi-criterion evaluation in PSO-CSM~\cite{wei2025}. Although these single-objective approaches yield valuable localized improvements, they address initialization, perturbation, and weighting in isolation, lacking a unified closed-loop architecture. Concurrently, multi-task evolutionary optimization has emerged as a promising paradigm for scaling feature selection to massive dimensions by exploring complementary continuous and discrete representations across collaborative subpopulations~\cite{mel2024}. Nevertheless, existing multi-task swarm frameworks leave critical structural vulnerabilities unresolved: continuous particles remain uniformly scattered without domain prior guidance, empirical feature-importance accumulation lacks exponential forgetting and elitist verification, and static objective scalarization persists throughout evolution.

To resolve these collective limitations, this paper proposes Knowledge-Guided Dual-Swarm Evolutionary Learning (KDS-EL). Methodologically, KDS-EL is structurally formulated not as an isolated particle swarm heuristic variant, but as a domain-knowledge-guided feature selection framework established upon a cooperative multi-task dual-swarm PSO backbone, cohesively coordinating prior knowledge injection, cross-iteration memory accumulation, targeted elite refinement, and dynamic objective scheduling into an end-to-end closed-loop learning system.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Prior-Guided Sparse Initialization with Adaptive Stagnation Recovery}: A hybrid seeding strategy that embeds continuous mutual-information soft priors into continuous decision variables without irreversible dimensional truncation, preserving full search-space accessibility while integrating Gaussian dispersion and an adaptive stagnation recovery mechanism to mitigate early evaluation waste without sacrificing diversity (Section~\ref{sec:mi}).
\item \textit{Knowledge-Guided Elite Refinement (KER)}: A targeted elite refinement strategy that dynamically harvests cross-iteration feature-importance feedback with exponential memory decay, directing elite solutions toward historically effective dimensions within a controlled generational evaluation overhead of $k_e \ll N$ candidates (Section~\ref{sec:ker}).
\item \textit{Two-Phase Weight Scheduling (TPS)}: A stage-aware objective scheduling mechanism that temporally decouples early aggressive feature pruning from late-stage accuracy refinement, overcoming the rigidity of static weighting (Section~\ref{sec:twophase}).
\item \textit{Comprehensive Empirical Benchmark Validation}: Extensive evaluations across 20 high-dimensional benchmark datasets spanning 500 to 54{,}675 features against nine representative competitor algorithms and intermediate configurations, rigorously validated through non-parametric statistical testing, component contribution analysis, and parametric sensitivity investigations (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that KDS-EL delivers competitive overall performance among evolutionary wrapper feature selection paradigms under comprehensive multi-criteria evaluation, demonstrating a consistent advantage in feature compactness alongside competitive benchmark-level optimization performance. Specifically, KDS-EL attains an omnibus mean internal cross-validation accuracy of 0.9545 under the standardized wrapper protocol while isolating an ultra-compact feature subset averaging merely 87.8 active features, representing an active dimension compression exceeding 98\%. Furthermore, despite evaluating inductive wrapper classification boundaries, KDS-EL achieves a marked 3.4-fold empirical acceleration over canonical dual-swarm search. Non-parametric Friedman rankings and post-hoc Holm--Bonferroni step-down verification confirm that KDS-EL establishes a statistically favorable omnibus ranking across the benchmark suite, achieving a balanced trade-off between predictive discrimination, extreme dimensional parsimony, and runtime efficiency.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} formulates the feature selection optimization problem and reviews related literature and background concepts. Section~\ref{sec:proposed} presents the proposed KDS-EL framework and formulates its three constituent mechanisms in detail. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents comprehensive experimental results, statistical tests, component contribution analysis, and parameter sensitivity investigations. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper and discusses prospective research directions.

\subsection{Problem formulation and search paradigms in feature selection}\label{sec:problem_formulation}

The essence of feature selection lies in combinatorial optimization, aiming to identify an optimal, minimal subset of informative features from a high-dimensional feature space to maximize classification performance while discarding redundant and uninformative dimensions~\cite{guyon2003,lili2017}. Formally, let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{f}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{samp}}}$ denote a supervised training dataset comprising $N_{\mathrm{samp}}$ instances, where $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ represents the full feature vector in the original $D$-dimensional space and $y_i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, C\}$ designates the discrete class label across $C$ categories. In discrete binary representations, a candidate feature subset is formulated as a binary decision indicator vector $\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^D$, defined as:
\begin{equation}
x_j = \begin{cases}
1, & \text{if feature } f_j \text{ is selected}, \\[2pt]
0, & \text{if feature } f_j \text{ is discarded}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:binary_decision}
\end{equation}

The primary objective of feature selection is to simultaneously minimize the classification error rate and the cardinality of the selected feature subset. In evolutionary wrapper feature selection, this multi-criterion goal is mathematically formalized as minimizing a scalarized single-objective fitness function $f(\mathbf{x})$~\cite{miao2024,deng2025nns}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^D} f(\mathbf{x}) = w_a \cdot \mathrm{ErrorRate}(\mathbf{x}) + w_f \cdot \left(\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_0}{D}\right),
\label{eq:fs_formulation}
\end{equation}
subject to $\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^D$ and $w_a + w_f = 1$, where $\mathrm{ErrorRate}(\mathbf{x})$ represents the cross-validation classification error evaluated on the masked feature subset $\{\mathbf{f}_i \odot \mathbf{x}\}$ using an inductive $k$-nearest neighbors ($k\text{-NN}$) learner, with $\odot$ denoting the Hadamard element-wise product. The term $\|\mathbf{x}\|_0$ denotes the number of active features, $\|\mathbf{x}\|_0 / D$ represents the feature selection ratio, and $w_a, w_f \in [0, 1]$ serve as trade-off coefficients balancing predictive accuracy against subset compactness. Because the cardinality of the discrete search space is $2^D$, evaluating all candidate subsets exhaustively is NP-hard and computationally intractable when $D \gg 10^3$. Traditional feature selection techniques are broadly categorized into three classical paradigms based on their evaluation criteria: filter, embedded, and wrapper methods~\cite{guyon2003,kohavi1997}. Specifically, \textit{filter methods} evaluate feature subsets based solely on intrinsic statistical or information-theoretic metrics---such as mutual information in mRMR~\cite{peng2005}, correlation in FCBF~\cite{yu2003}, or centrality scores~\cite{roffo2016}---independently of any classification algorithm. While filter methods are computationally lightweight with $\mathcal{O}(D N_{\mathrm{samp}} + D \log D)$ complexity, their classifier-agnostic evaluation and rigid hard truncation permanently discard weakly relevant features that exhibit strong collective synergy when combined. Conversely, \textit{embedded methods} perform feature selection natively during classifier training by embedding sparsity constraints into model parameter estimation, such as $L_1$-regularized regression in Bolasso~\cite{bach2008} or tree-based ensemble importance in FS-GBDT~\cite{zhang2021fsgbdt}. Although embedded methods bridge computational efficiency and predictive feedback, they remain architecturally bound to specific learners and frequently encounter performance degradation under severe sample scarcity where $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. In contrast, \textit{wrapper methods} utilize a target learning algorithm as a black-box evaluator, assessing candidate feature masks directly through cross-validation predictive error feedback~\cite{kohavi1997,miao2024}. By tightly coupling subset evaluation with classifier feedback, wrappers naturally capture complex non-linear feature interactions and yield superior classification accuracy.

To navigate the discrete $2^D$ combinatorial space in wrapper feature selection without gradient information, Evolutionary Computation paradigms---including Genetic Algorithms~\cite{huang2006}, Ant Colony Optimization~\cite{aghdam2009}, Artificial Bee Colony~\cite{karaboga2007}, and Swarm Intelligence~\cite{xue2016survey,liang2025}---have emerged as dominant search heuristics. To combat the curse of dimensionality in small-sample high-dimensional data, various advanced single-objective evolutionary strategies have been introduced. For instance, self-adaptive differential evolution with adaptive weighting (SaWDE)~\cite{wang2022} dynamically balances classification error against subset cardinality; feature-weight particle swarm optimization (FWPSO)~\cite{wangjia2022} injects offline feature weights to steer velocity updates; importance-guided particle swarm optimization (IGPSO)~\cite{xuezhang2024} employs two-stage ANOVA F-statistic permutation contrast scores to bias particle movements; and particle swarm optimization with comprehensive scoring mechanism (PSO-CSM)~\cite{wei2025} leverages multi-criterion feature evaluation to restrict search trajectories. Despite these advancements, standard single-objective evolutionary wrappers face severe structural bottlenecks in ultra-high-dimensional spaces where $D \gg 10^3$. Uniform random initialization scatters individuals indiscriminately across the search space, generating overly dense candidate masks that overburden classifier evaluations and provoke premature overfitting. Furthermore, stochastic variation operators treat feature perturbations as memoryless trials, discarding historical feature-importance feedback across generations. Finally, static or coarsely tuned objective weighting enforces rigid trade-offs throughout evolution, failing to adapt to the distinct requirements of early-stage dimensional reduction and late-stage accuracy refinement.

\subsection{Evolutionary swarm intelligence and discrete feature selection dynamics}\label{sec:pso}

Standard particle swarm optimization, originally introduced by Kennedy and Eberhart~\cite{kennedy1995}, is a population-based stochastic meta-heuristic inspired by the collective foraging behaviors of bird flocks and fish schools. A swarm consists of $N$ candidate particles navigating a $D$-dimensional continuous solution space $\mathbb{R}^D$. At iteration $t$, each particle $i$ is characterized by a continuous position vector $\mathbf{p}_i^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and an associated velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_i^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, steered dynamically by its personal historical best position $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and the swarm's global best position $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. The velocity and position updates of particle $i$ at iteration $t+1$ are formulated as follows~\cite{shi1998}:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{v}_i^{(t+1)} &= \omega \mathbf{v}_i^{(t)} + c_1 r_1 \bigl(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i} - \mathbf{p}_i^{(t)}\bigr) + c_2 r_2 \bigl(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}} - \mathbf{p}_i^{(t)}\bigr), \label{eq:pso_velocity} \\
\mathbf{p}_i^{(t+1)} &= \mathbf{p}_i^{(t)} + \mathbf{v}_i^{(t+1)}, \label{eq:pso_position}
\end{align}
where $\omega \in [0, 1]$ denotes the inertia weight controlling momentum retention, $c_1, c_2 > 0$ are the cognitive and social acceleration coefficients governing the attraction toward individual and collective best experiences, and $r_1, r_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ are independent uniform random factors providing stochastic exploration capability.

To adapt continuous PSO to the combinatorial nature of binary feature selection where candidate solutions are represented as binary indicator masks $\mathbf{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}^D$, Kennedy and Eberhart~\cite{kennedy1997} proposed Binary PSO (BPSO). In BPSO, continuous velocities are mapped into selection probabilities via a Sigmoid transfer function:
\begin{equation}
S(v_{i,j}^{(t+1)}) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\bigl(-v_{i,j}^{(t+1)}\bigr)},
\label{eq:bpsosigmoid}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding binary decision variable $x_{i,j}^{(t+1)}$ is sampled probabilistically as:
\begin{equation}
x_{i,j}^{(t+1)} = \begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } r_{i,j} < S(v_{i,j}^{(t+1)}),\\[2pt]
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bpso_sampling}
\end{equation}
where $r_{i,j} \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$ is an independent uniform random threshold. Although BPSO exhibits fast convergence on low-dimensional benchmarks, it encounters severe exploration-exploitation dilemmas when applied to ultra-high-dimensional biomedical domains where $D \gg 10^3$. Because the Sigmoid function satisfies $S(0) = 0.5$, continuous velocities hovering near zero inevitably yield an expected active feature density of approximately $D/2$. In typical biomedical datasets containing $D \ge 10{,}000$ dimensions, this stochastic mapping generates candidate subsets with more than $5{,}000$ active features, heavily overloading wrapper classifier evaluations with $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 d_{\mathrm{selected}})$ complexity and triggering severe overfitting under small sample sizes where $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. Furthermore, continuous stochastic sampling introduces high generational variance: even when velocity vectors stabilize, candidate feature masks continue to jitter stochastically, causing particle trajectories to degrade into memoryless random walks across the combinatorial space.

To overcome the random drift of standard binarization in high-dimensional feature selection, diverse single-objective meta-heuristic algorithms have recently been proposed. Evolutionary filter methods, such as DENCA-SO~\cite{hancer2023}, employ neighborhood component feature selection (NCFS) continuous weights within differential evolution operators. By evaluating candidate subsets using algebraic surrogate costs rather than wrapper classifiers, DENCA-SO achieves rapid wall-clock execution; however, because its optimization process remains entirely decoupled from inductive classification margins, it suffers from severe feature redundancy and retains over 48\% of original dimensions on biomedical benchmarks. Concurrently, advanced binary swarm optimizers seek to enhance discrete exploration. For instance, bIRIME~\cite{su2024} incorporates dynamic escape energy, composite mutation strategies, and opposition-based restart mechanisms to mitigate premature convergence, whereas BinCOA~\cite{shikoun2024} couples refractive opposition-based learning with crossed horizontal and vertical search operations. Nevertheless, these multi-operator strategies incur prohibitive computational burdens: generating and evaluating multiple trial vectors per agent scales wrapper classifier calls to three or four times the population size per generation, causing execution times to escalate beyond 2,000~seconds on large-sample benchmarks (Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}). Crucially, in ultra-high dimensions without proactive pruning guidance, stochastic binarization traps these algorithms near the uninformative 50\% dimensionality attractor, preserving thousands of redundant features. On the other hand, wrapper methods employing scalarized fitness formulations---such as SaWDE~\cite{wang2022}, FWPSO~\cite{wangjia2022}, and PSO-CSM~\cite{wei2025}---enforce fixed static objective weights throughout evolution. This static weighting creates a fundamental evolutionary phase misalignment: the search process inherently requires aggressive feature pruning during early exploration to rapidly contract vast noise subspaces, whereas delicate decision-boundary fine-tuning is required during late-stage exploitation to maximize predictive accuracy.

\subsection{Multi-task evolutionary learning and dual-swarm search}\label{sec:mel}

Evolutionary Multi-Task Optimization (EMTO) solves multiple distinct optimization tasks simultaneously by facilitating inter-task knowledge transfer across unified representations~\cite{zhang2021mtl}. In feature selection, EMTO frameworks divide the population into collaborative subpopulations exploring complementary search subspaces~\cite{zhang2021minion,chen2020emfs,chen2021emfs,he2022,liu2022}. Notably, Wang et al.~\cite{mel2024} established a dual-subpopulation cooperative multi-task framework where a population of $N$ particles is partitioned into collaborative subpopulations $\mathcal{P}_1$ and $\mathcal{P}_2$, each comprising $N/2$ individuals operating on complementary representations. Specifically, \textit{Subpopulation~1} executes continuous swarm exploration in $[0, 1]^D$, updating particle velocities and positions through PSO dynamics augmented with an inter-task knowledge transfer channel directing particles toward the historical global best position $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best},2} \in \{0, 1\}^D$ of Subpopulation~2:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}_{1,i}^{(t+1)} = \omega \mathbf{v}_{1,i}^{(t)} &+ c_1 \mathbf{r}_1 \odot \bigl(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},1,i} - \mathbf{x}_{1,i}^{(t)}\bigr) \\
&+ c_2 \mathbf{r}_2 \odot \bigl(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best},1} - \mathbf{x}_{1,i}^{(t)}\bigr) + c_3 \mathbf{r}_3 \odot \bigl(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best},2} - \mathbf{x}_{1,i}^{(t)}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mel_sub1}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_{1,i}^{(t)} \in [0, 1]^D$ and $\mathbf{v}_{1,i}^{(t)} \in [v_{\min}, v_{\max}]^D$ denote the continuous position and velocity of particle $i$, $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},1,i}$ and $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best},1}$ represent personal and local bests, $\omega \in [0, 1]$ is the inertia weight, $c_1, c_2, c_3 > 0$ are acceleration coefficients, $\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2, \mathbf{r}_3 \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)^D$, and $\odot$ denotes the Hadamard product. Continuous positions are binarized into candidate selection masks via transfer mapping. Concurrently, \textit{Subpopulation~2} performs discrete sampling from a shared importance vector $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{freq}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ accumulating historical feature selection frequencies across candidate solutions. Both subpopulations evaluate candidate subsets via a shared scalar fitness function similar to Eq.~(\ref{eq:fs_formulation}) with fixed static weights $\alpha, \beta \in [0, 1]$ satisfying $\alpha + \beta = 1$.

While multi-task swarm learning establishes the utility of cross-task knowledge transfer, dual-swarm dynamics encounter critical structural bottlenecks in ultra-high-dimensional and sample-scarce regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. First, continuous particles in $\mathcal{P}_1$ are initialized uniformly without domain guidance, scattering early exploration and squandering wrapper evaluations across uninformative subspaces, motivating the prior initialization and adaptive recovery strategies in Section~\ref{sec:mi}. Second, accumulating historical selection frequencies without exponential forgetting or elite fitness verification prematurely biases discrete sampling in $\mathcal{P}_2$, necessitating the differential importance learning and targeted elite refinement mechanisms in Section~\ref{sec:ker}. Third, enforcing static objective weights creates an evolutionary phase misalignment between early coarse pruning and late accuracy refinement, resolved by the dynamic Two-Phase Weight Scheduling strategy in Section~\ref{sec:twophase}. In response, KDS-EL establishes a closed-loop evolutionary learning framework tailored for high-dimensional feature selection.

\subsection{Knowledge-guided search and taxonomic positioning}\label{sec:knowledge_guided}

Integrating domain knowledge into meta-heuristics effectively mitigates the blindness of black-box combinatorial search~\cite{ye2026,mimpode2025,deng2025nns}. In high-dimensional feature selection, Mutual Information (MI), rooted in Shannon's information theory~\cite{cover1991}, quantifies non-linear dependency between continuous feature variables $f_j \in \mathbb{R}$ and discrete class labels $Y \in \{1, \dots, C\}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I(f_j; Y) &= H(f_j) - H(f_j \mid Y) \\
&= \sum_{c=1}^C P(Y = c) \int_{\mathbb{R}} p(f_j \mid Y = c) \log_2 \frac{p(f_j \mid Y = c)}{p(f_j)} \, df_j,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mi}
\end{equation}
where $P(Y = c)$ is the class prior probability, while $p(f_j)$ and $p(f_j \mid Y = c)$ denote the marginal and conditional density functions of continuous feature $f_j$. To avoid bin-width sensitivity in empirical applications with unknown distributions, non-parametric nearest-neighbor estimators---specifically the Kraskov--St{\"o}gbauer--Grassberger (KSG) estimator~\cite{kraskov2004} extended by Ross~\cite{ross2014}---directly evaluate non-linear dependencies. Crucially, in ultra-high-dimensional and sample-scarce regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly outnumber observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$, continuous estimators inevitably incur finite-sample estimation variance, producing ranking noise. Conventional filter methods~\cite{peng2005,deng2025nns} evaluate mutual information in $\mathcal{O}(D N_{\mathrm{samp}} + D \log D)$ time but enforce rigid hard truncation, retaining only the top-$k$ ranked features. This hard cut-off fundamentally ignores multi-feature synergy: weakly individual features that are collectively informative when combined are permanently discarded, while false positives elevated by finite-sample noise are irreversibly locked into the candidate pool.

Existing knowledge-guided evolutionary methods primarily adopt one-shot offline injection via either static subspace pre-selection~\cite{mimpode2025,deng2025nns} or static velocity biasing~\cite{wangjia2022}. While beneficial in narrowing early search, one-shot injection lacks online adaptability: the static prior remains frozen and cannot assimilate iterative trial-and-error feedback from inductive classification margins. Conversely, online surrogate-assisted approaches---such as neural network surrogates or Gaussian processes~\cite{liu2024}---attempt dynamic importance learning but incur prohibitive retraining latencies and suffer severe model misspecification under sample-scarce regimes where observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$ cannot constrain high-capacity surrogates.

To resolve the limitations of rigid static priors and heavyweight surrogates, KDS-EL establishes a closed-loop, knowledge-guided evolutionary learning framework unifying offline domain priors, online memory harvesting, and stage-aware objective scheduling. Specifically, normalized mutual information scores are embedded as continuous Gaussian-perturbed soft seeds, preserving the complete search space while steering the swarm toward an ultra-sparse initial basin, complemented by an adaptive recovery mechanism to restore diversity upon stagnation (Section~\ref{sec:mi}). Throughout evolution, cross-iteration fitness feedback is accumulated into a differential importance vector $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ governed by exponential forgetting, enabling the KER strategy to execute targeted, directional elite flips within a controlled evaluation budget (Section~\ref{sec:ker}). Finally, a dynamic Two-Phase Weight Scheduling strategy temporally decouples early coarse pruning from late boundary refinement, achieving simultaneous gains in classification accuracy, feature compactness, and runtime efficiency (Section~\ref{sec:twophase}). \subsection{The framework of KDS-EL}\label{sec:framework}

The macro data-flow architecture and problem mapping of KDS-EL for high-dimensional feature selection are systematically illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:kds_el_dataflow}. An initial population of continuous positions $\mathbf{p}_i \in [0, 1]^D$ is generated via hybrid prior seeding and deterministically mapped into sparse binary candidate masks $\mathbf{x}_i \in \{0, 1\}^D$ via Heaviside thresholding. Throughout optimization, these candidate subsets are transferred to the KDS-EL optimization engine, which coordinates collaborative dual-swarm co-evolution between subpopulations $\mathcal{P}_1$ and $\mathcal{P}_2$, dynamic Two-Phase Weight Scheduling, wrapper classification evaluations, cumulative feature-importance harvesting into memory vector $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, and targeted elite refinement in closed-loop synergy. Upon satisfying the termination criterion, the framework isolates the optimal compact feature subset $\mathbf{x}^{\star}$ with cardinality $d^{\star} = \|\mathbf{x}^{\star}\|_0 \ll D$ alongside its cross-validated predictive performance.

The execution logic and operational flowchart across evolutionary generations are depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:kds_el_flowchart}. In KDS-EL, candidate solutions operate under a continuous-discrete dual representation: continuous coordinates reside in the unit hypercube $\mathbf{P} = [\mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_N]^T \in [0, 1]^{N \times D}$, while the evaluated feature subsets across $D$ dimensions are formally represented as an $N \times D$ binary decision matrix $\mathbf{X}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_1 \\
\mathbf{x}_2 \\
\vdots \\
\mathbf{x}_N
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
x_{1,1} & x_{1,2} & \cdots & x_{1,D} \\
x_{2,1} & x_{2,2} & \cdots & x_{2,D} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
x_{N,1} & x_{N,2} & \cdots & x_{N,D}
\end{bmatrix} \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times D},
\label{eq:pop_matrix}
\end{equation}
where the $i$-th row vector $\mathbf{x}_i = [x_{i,1}, x_{i,2}, \dots, x_{i,D}] \in \{0, 1\}^D$ denotes an individual candidate solution, with $x_{i,j} = 1$ indicating that feature $f_j$ is selected and $x_{i,j} = 0$ indicating exclusion. The mapping from continuous particle position $\mathbf{p}_i = [p_{i,1}, \dots, p_{i,D}] \in [0, 1]^D$ to binary decision mask $\mathbf{x}_i$ is governed by a deterministic threshold condition $x_{i,j} = \mathbb{I}(p_{i,j} \ge \theta)$, where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ denotes the indicator function and $\theta \in (0.5, 1)$ represents the binarization cut-off threshold.

To balance global exploration and local exploitation in sparse high-dimensional spaces, the swarm is partitioned into two collaborative subpopulations $\mathcal{P}_1$ and $\mathcal{P}_2$, each comprising $N/2$ particles, leveraging a collaborative continuous-discrete multi-task learning architecture~\cite{mel2024}. As delineated in the upper dashed sub-panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:kds_el_flowchart}, Subpopulation $\mathcal{P}_1$ functions as a continuous explorer, executing continuous velocity--position updates with an inter-task knowledge transfer channel guided by the global best solution $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best},2}$ of $\mathcal{P}_2$. Concurrently, Subpopulation $\mathcal{P}_2$ acts as a discrete exploiter, conducting discrete probability-driven feature sampling informed by the accumulated feature-importance landscape $\mathbf{W}$. Compared with conventional single-population architectures, this collaborative co-evolutionary design establishes complementary search dynamics: continuous swarm movement preserves momentum to traverse unvisited spaces, while discrete sampling focuses evaluations on verified high-relevance dimensions, effectively mitigating premature stagnation.

During iterative optimization, KDS-EL orchestrates three complementary mechanisms into an integrated closed loop. First, population initialization injects Gaussian-perturbed mutual information priors to establish an ultra-sparse candidate basin, while an adaptive re-initialization mechanism restores swarm diversity whenever search progress halts. Second, differential fitness improvements across generations are accumulated into a continuous importance vector $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ under exponential memory decay, actively steering the KER strategy to execute targeted directional bit flips under a greedy pairwise in-place replacement rule, as illustrated in the lower dashed sub-panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:kds_el_flowchart}. Third, the dynamic TPS strategy temporally shifts objective weights across evolutionary stages, enforcing aggressive feature pruning during the early phase and fine-grained accuracy tuning during the late phase. Architecturally, KDS-EL establishes an orthogonal spatio-temporal hierarchy: the collaborative dual-swarm structure governs spatial exploration across complementary continuous and discrete domains, whereas the dynamic TPS strategy orchestrates temporal search progression across decoupled pruning and refinement phases. By continuously cycling through these coordinated stages, KDS-EL systematically prunes non-informative dimensions while progressively refining compact feature subsets.

\subsection{Population initialization and adaptive recovery}\label{sec:mi}

In ultra-high-dimensional wrapper feature selection, standard uniform random initialization samples continuous particle coordinates uniformly across the unit hypercube, activating each candidate feature with an expected probability of $0.50$ and collapsing candidate subsets into the dense dimensionality attractor characterized by cardinality $d \approx D/2$. In high-dimensional regimes where $D \gg 10^3$, evaluating these dense candidate masks forces wrapper classifiers to compute pairwise distances across thousands of non-informative dimensions at a computational complexity scaling as $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 d)$, which squanders early function evaluation budgets and precipitates severe classifier overfitting under limited sample observations where $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. To establish an informative yet diverse initial swarm while systematically mitigating mid-evolution search stagnation, KDS-EL introduces a prior-guided sparse population initialization strategy coupled with an adaptive stagnation recovery mechanism, as systematically illustrated across the four operational stages in Fig.~\ref{fig:mi_seeding}.

\subsubsection{Prior-guided sparse population initialization}

Given the training dataset instance $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{f}_k, y_k)\}_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{samp}}}$ with continuous feature vectors $\mathbf{f}_k = [f_{k,1}, \dots, f_{k,D}] \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and discrete class labels $y_k \in \{1, \dots, C\}$, the non-linear dependencies between individual candidate dimensions and target categories are quantified through continuous-discrete mutual information~\cite{ross2014}. To handle continuous real-valued inputs without relying on arbitrary data discretization, the mutual information score $\hat{I}(f_j; Y)$ for each feature $j \in \{1, \dots, D\}$ is computed via the non-parametric $k$-nearest neighbors KSG estimator~\cite{kraskov2004,ross2014}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}(f_j; Y) = \psi(N_{\mathrm{samp}}) - \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{samp}}} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{samp}}} \psi(N_{y_k}) + \psi(k_{\mathrm{nn}}) - \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{samp}}} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{samp}}} \psi(n_{f_j, k}),
\label{eq:mi_ksg}
\end{equation}
where $\psi(\cdot)$ denotes the digamma function, $N_{y_k}$ is the count of training samples sharing class label $y_k$, $k_{\mathrm{nn}} = 3$ is the neighborhood parameter in the joint continuous-discrete space, and $n_{f_j, k}$ is the count of data points whose marginal continuous distance to sample $k$ along feature $f_j$ is strictly smaller than the distance to its $k_{\mathrm{nn}}$-th neighbor in the class-conditioned subspace. To establish a standardized probability profile across heterogeneous datasets, these raw mutual information scores are normalized into a prior relevance vector $\mathbf{m} = [m_1, \dots, m_D]^T \in [0, 1]^D$:
\begin{equation}
m_j = \frac{\hat{I}(f_j; Y) - \min_{1 \le l \le D} \hat{I}(f_l; Y)}{\max_{1 \le l \le D} \hat{I}(f_l; Y) - \min_{1 \le l \le D} \hat{I}(f_l; Y) + \epsilon}, \quad j = 1, \dots, D,
\label{eq:mi_norm}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon > 0$ is a small numerical constant preventing division by zero.

To guarantee strict methodological integrity and safeguard against data leakage, the filter prior calculation is architecturally restricted to the training partition presented to the optimizer. In an inductive machine learning deployment where an external unseen test partition $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$ or an outer nested evaluation loop is prescribed, the prior relevance vector $\mathbf{m}$ and the subsequent evolutionary wrapper search are executed strictly on the training partition $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}$, ensuring that unseen validation samples remain completely unobserved throughout all search stages. In the standardized benchmark optimization protocol adopted in this work (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), $\mathcal{D}$ serves as the complete benchmark problem instance, within which an internal 5-fold cross-validation scheme functions as the wrapper fitness evaluation surrogate.

To prevent population homogenization while guiding search trajectories toward informative dimensions, candidate particles are initialized through a hybrid seeding scheme parameterized by a seeding ratio $\rho \in (0, 1)$, as illustrated in Step~1 of Fig.~\ref{fig:mi_seeding}. Continuous position vectors $\mathbf{p}_i = [p_{i,1}, \dots, p_{i,D}] \in [0, 1]^D$ are constructed as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_i = \begin{cases}
\mathrm{clip}\left(\mathbf{m} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i, \; 0, \; 1\right), & 1 \le i \le \lfloor \rho N \rfloor, \\[4pt]
\mathbf{u}_i, & \lfloor \rho N \rfloor < i \le N,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:mi_seeding}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_D)$ represents zero-mean Gaussian perturbation with standard deviation $\sigma$, $\mathbf{u}_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)^D$ provides uniform stochastic coverage across unguided dimensions, and $\mathrm{clip}(z, 0, 1) = \max(0, \min(1, z))$ restricts continuous coordinates to the unit hypercube.

Continuous position vectors $\mathbf{p}_i$ are subsequently transformed into binary decision vectors $\mathbf{x}_i = [x_{i,1}, \dots, x_{i,D}] \in \{0, 1\}^D$ via a deterministic Heaviside step mapping with threshold $\theta \in (0.5, 1)$:
\begin{equation}
x_{i,j} = \begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } p_{i,j} \ge \theta, \\[2pt]
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases} \quad j = 1, \dots, D.
\label{eq:binarize}
\end{equation}
By setting the binarization threshold to $\theta = 0.60 \in (0.5, 1)$, candidate features are activated with an expected probability of $0.40$ for unguided particles, breaking the symmetric $0.50$ expectation. For prior-seeded particles, this threshold restricts initial activation strictly to dimensions exceeding the 60th percentile of normalized relevance, producing a sparse initial population that mitigates initial search blindness and accelerates early convergence toward discriminative subspaces.

\textit{Estimation robustness and mitigation of the Winner's curse under sample scarcity}: In ultra-high-dimensional and sample-scarce regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed sample observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$ by two to three orders of magnitude, non-parametric entropy estimation on limited observations inherently exhibits non-trivial finite-sample variance. Under extensive dimensional testing, stochastic sample alignment across thousands of candidate features may cause irrelevant noise dimensions to obtain spuriously elevated mutual information estimates---a classic manifestation of the Winner's curse in high-dimensional screening. If subjected to standard Min-Max normalization, such estimation fluctuations risk being amplified toward $m_j \approx 1$, potentially injecting false-positive biases into the initial population.

Crucially, KDS-EL establishes a multi-layered architectural defense that insulates the evolutionary search against noisy prior contamination. First, KDS-EL fundamentally departs from conventional filter pre-selectors by rejecting irreversible hard feature truncation; rather than permanently discarding unselected dimensions, the normalized vector $\mathbf{m}$ establishes a continuous soft prior manifold, maintaining the complete $\mathbb{R}^D$ feature space fully accessible throughout optimization. Second, the hybrid initialization in \eqref{eq:mi_seeding} injects Gaussian dispersion $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_D)$ around $\mathbf{m}$ while maintaining an unguided sub-swarm $\mathbf{u}_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)^D$ across $(1 - \rho)N$ particles, preventing premature population collapse into false-positive empirical peaks. Third, every candidate subset is immediately subjected to wrapper cross-validation classification feedback; because spurious noise features fail to sustain predictive consistency across cross-validation folds, they generate zero or negative fitness differentials. Under the exponential memory decay governed by decay factor $\gamma$ in \eqref{eq:decay}, their transient weights in the cumulative feature-importance vector $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ rapidly decay to zero. Consequently, the knowledge-guided elite refinement mechanism swiftly purges these non-contributing dimensions in favor of synergistic, predictive features. Finally, should the swarm become temporarily trapped in empirical local attractors, the adaptive stagnation recovery routine (Section~\ref{sec:reinit}) re-samples the stagnant subpopulation under fresh stochastic perturbations, ensuring robust long-term exploratory self-correction.

\subsubsection{Adaptive stagnation recovery}\label{sec:reinit}

In iterative swarm search over ultra-high-dimensional fitness landscapes, population diversity inevitably decays as particles converge toward shared historical attractors. As evolutionary generations progress, particle velocities decelerate toward zero, causing the swarm to cluster tightly within confined local basins. In high-dimensional regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed sample observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$, such velocity decay precipitates premature search stagnation, trapping the optimizer in sub-optimal feature combinations governed by empirical noise. Conventional meta-heuristics frequently attempt to combat stagnation via uniform random re-initialization or heavy-tailed mutation perturbations, such as Cauchy mutations or opposition-based learning~\cite{su2024,shikoun2024}. However, in ultra-high-dimensional spaces, uniform random restarts inevitably collapse candidate solutions back into dense, uninformative subspaces, squandering subsequent wrapper evaluations on non-informative noise dimensions. Similarly, blind heavy-tailed mutations in $2^D$ combinatorial space perturb candidate bit positions without domain guidance, dismantling carefully discovered, highly cooperative feature combinations with an overwhelming probability exceeding $99.9\%$. Furthermore, classical opposition-based learning strategies invert binary masks across all dimensions, transforming sparse subsets into massive complementary masks of cardinality $D - d \approx 10^4$, thereby exacerbating classifier overfitting and computational latency. Consequently, sustaining exploratory diversity in ultra-high dimensions requires an asymmetric recovery mechanism capable of injecting targeted stochastic exploration while preserving verified elite solutions.

To resolve this structural dilemma without incurring catastrophic forgetting or dimensional bloat, KDS-EL introduces an adaptive stagnation recovery mechanism. The adaptiveness of this mechanism stems from its feedback-driven, on-demand activation: unlike classical heuristic restarts that disrupt search trajectories on fixed generational schedules regardless of convergence status, KDS-EL couples recovery execution dynamically to runtime fitness stagnation feedback. Throughout generational search, a stagnation counter $t_{\mathrm{stag}}$ monitors the consecutive generational span during which the global-best fitness fails to achieve a strict numerical decrease. As long as the swarm sustains productive descent, the counter $t_{\mathrm{stag}}$ is continuously reset, leaving ongoing trajectories undisturbed; recovery is triggered strictly on demand when $t_{\mathrm{stag}} \ge \tau$, where $\tau \in \mathbb{N}^+$ serves as a preset patience parameter regulating detection sensitivity. When this condition is met, search stagnation is formally identified, triggering an asymmetric bipartite population bisection, as illustrated in Step~3 of Fig.~\ref{fig:mi_seeding}. Specifically, candidate particles are ranked in ascending order according to their historical personal-best fitness values $f(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}, \mathbf{w}(t))$. To safeguard converged, highly discriminative feature combinations, the superior half of the swarm comprising $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ particles is preserved intact, maintaining the optimization memory accumulated along successful search trajectories. Concurrently, the lower-performing half comprising the remaining $N - \lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ stagnant particles has both its current continuous positions $\mathbf{p}_i$ and personal-best memory vectors $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}$ refreshed by re-sampling around the continuous prior relevance vector $\mathbf{m} \in [0, 1]^D$ under Gaussian perturbation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{p}_i &\leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\left(\mathbf{m} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i, \; 0, \; 1\right), \quad \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i} \leftarrow \mathbf{p}_i, \\
x_{i,j} &\leftarrow \mathbb{I}(p_{i,j} \ge \theta), \quad \forall j = 1, \dots, D,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reinit_formula}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}_D)$ represents zero-mean Gaussian perturbation with standard deviation $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^+$, $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function, $\theta \in (0.5, 1)$ denotes the cut-off binarization threshold, and $\mathrm{clip}(z, 0, 1) = \max(0, \min(1, z))$ restricts continuous coordinates to the unit hypercube.

Immediately following position refreshing, the binarized candidate masks $\mathbf{x}_i$ of the refreshed subpopulation are evaluated via the internal cross-validation wrapper under the current phase objective weight vector $\mathbf{w}(t)$, as depicted in Step~4 of Fig.~\ref{fig:mi_seeding}. The refreshed personal-best fitness records are updated to $f(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}, \mathbf{w}(t)) \leftarrow f(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{w}(t))$, allowing newly sampled candidates to enter subsequent global-best competition alongside the preserved elite half, while the stagnation counter is reset to zero ($t_{\mathrm{stag}} \leftarrow 0$). Crucially, this bipartite design bounds the additional computational overhead to at most $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ wrapper evaluations, which are invoked strictly on demand rather than continuously incurred across every generation. Furthermore, because re-sampling is anchored to the continuous prior relevance vector $\mathbf{m}$ rather than uniform noise, newly generated candidate masks remain structurally sparse, entirely circumventing unguided dimensional expansion. When coupled with the exponential memory forgetting of non-contributing dimensions in the cumulative feature-importance vector $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, this adaptive recovery mechanism breaks exploratory deadlocks, injects fresh variance into dormant subspaces, and propels the swarm toward globally competitive, sparse feature subsets.

\subsection{Cumulative importance learning and elite refinement}\label{sec:importance}\label{sec:ker}

To overcome the search amnesia and destructive perturbation bottlenecks of conventional meta-heuristics, KDS-EL establishes a closed-loop knowledge learning mechanism comprising online importance accumulation and targeted directional elite refinement.

In ultra-high-dimensional feature selection where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed sample observations, traditional evolutionary wrappers rely on undirected stochastic bit flips with a uniform mutation probability $p_{\mathrm{mut}} = 1/D$. Under this blind perturbation regime, meta-heuristics inevitably suffer from two structural search lesions. First, when an established, highly informative feature with active status $x_{e,j} = 1$ is blindly mutated to $0$, delicate multi-feature synergies are dismantled, inducing severe classification degradation. Second, when an uninformative background noise feature with inactive state $x_{e,j} = 0$ is randomly flipped to $1$, the candidate subset expands unnecessarily, escalating evaluation latency and precipitating classifier overfitting. Because the probability of randomly hitting informative dimensions within a $2^D$ combinatorial neighborhood is asymptotically infinitesimal, scaling as $\mathcal{O}(1/D)$, undirected mutation squanders function evaluations on destructive trials. Furthermore, conventional meta-heuristics exhibit severe search amnesia, as historical fitness feedback from intermediate iterations is discarded immediately after particle position updates.

\subsubsection{Online feature importance learning}

In ultra-high-dimensional wrapper feature selection, standard evolutionary algorithms suffer from severe search amnesia. Although thousands of candidate feature subsets are iteratively synthesized and evaluated via cross-validation, historical fitness differentials produced by coordinate transitions are discarded immediately following generational updates. Consequently, evolutionary trajectories remain memoryless, repeatedly re-evaluating uninformative dimensions and barren combinatorial regions. To bridge generational evaluations with cumulative historical learning, KDS-EL maintains a persistent cumulative directional credit vector $\mathbf{W} = [W_1, \dots, W_D] \in \mathbb{R}^D$, initialized to $\mathbf{0}$, which systematically records the directional contribution of each candidate dimension toward classification discriminability.

At generation $t$, whenever an updated candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)} \in \{0, 1\}^D$ achieves a strict fitness improvement over its historical personal best $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i} \in \{0, 1\}^D$, the algorithm isolates the differential feature set $\mathcal{C}_i^{(t)} \subset \{1, \dots, D\}$ containing coordinates whose binary states were altered during the evolutionary update:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_i^{(t)} = \left\{j \in \{1, \dots, D\} \;\middle|\; x_{i,j}^{(t)} \neq p_{\mathrm{best},i,j}\right\}.
\label{eq:changed_features}
\end{equation}
Let $\delta_i^{(t)} = f(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}, \mathbf{w}(t)) - f(\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)}, \mathbf{w}(t))$ denote the generational fitness differential evaluated under dynamic weight vector $\mathbf{w}(t)$. To distinguish beneficial feature alterations from detrimental ones, the algorithm executes bidirectional credit updating. When candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)}$ achieves a strict fitness improvement over its historical personal best with $\delta_i^{(t)} > 0$, each altered coordinate $j \in \mathcal{C}_i^{(t)}$ receives a directional reinforcement scaled by $\delta_i^{(t)}$:
\begin{equation}
W_j \leftarrow W_j + \delta_i^{(t)} \cdot (2 x_{i,j}^{(t)} - 1), \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{C}_i^{(t)},
\label{eq:importance_update}
\end{equation}
where the polarity factor $(2 x_{i,j}^{(t)} - 1) \in \{-1, +1\}$ yields $+1$ when feature $j$ was newly activated with $x_{i,j}^{(t)} = 1$ to achieve the superior solution, reinforcing its importance score, and yields $-1$ when feature $j$ was successfully pruned with $x_{i,j}^{(t)} = 0$, penalizing its inclusion. Conversely, when candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)}$ fails to improve upon the personal best, characterized by $\Delta_i^{(t)} = f(\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)}, \mathbf{w}(t)) - f(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}, \mathbf{w}(t)) \ge 0$, the coordinates altered during the unrewarded transition incur a symmetric penalty:
\begin{equation}
W_j \leftarrow W_j - \Delta_i^{(t)} \cdot (2 x_{i,j}^{(t)} - 1), \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{C}_i^{(t)}.
\label{eq:importance_penalty}
\end{equation}
This bidirectional credit-penalty dynamic ensures that beneficial transitions reinforce feature utility while deteriorating moves dynamically suppress counterproductive coordinate changes.

Because candidate subsets become progressively sparser across evolution, unconstrained accumulation would allow early-discovered, coarsely predictive dimensions to dominate $\mathbf{W}$ indefinitely. Such historical inertia suppresses emergent synergistic feature interactions that only become apparent once redundant dimensions are pruned. To reconcile long-term knowledge retention with responsiveness to emergent feature synergies, an exponential memory decay is applied at the conclusion of each generation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \gamma \cdot \mathbf{W},
\label{eq:decay}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ denotes the continuous memory retention factor governing the exponential forgetting rate. Consequently, dimensions that consistently yield fitness gains sustain prominent positive scores, whereas transient or uninformative coordinates undergo geometric attenuation toward zero. Crucially, as $\gamma \to 1$, historical search credit accumulates without forgetting, risking exploratory fixation; conversely, as $\gamma \to 0$, memory collapses into single-step myopic feedback. Maintaining $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ ensures that the importance vector provides a calibrated probabilistic landscape that directly steers discrete sampling in Subpopulation $\mathcal{P}_2$ and guides the directional elite refinement strategy.

\subsubsection{Knowledge-guided elite refinement}

In ultra-high-dimensional regimes where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed sample observations, uniform bit-flipping degenerates into blind trial-and-error, disrupting synergistic feature coalitions and precipitating search stagnation. Moreover, applying local search across all particles incurs prohibitive computational latency scaling with swarm size $N$. To overcome this combinatorial dilemma, the KER strategy exploits the continuous feature-importance landscape $\mathbf{W}$ to steer directional bit inversions strictly on a compact elite echelon $k_e \ll N$, concentrating search momentum on high-potential dimensions while bounding evaluation overhead to exactly $k_e$ fitness calculations per generation.

\begin{algorithm}[!htb]
\small
\caption{Knowledge-guided elite refinement (KER) strategy}
\label{alg:ker}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Elite candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_e$, importance vector $\mathbf{W}$, budget $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$, exploration probability $p_{\mathrm{rand}}$, objective weights $\mathbf{w}(t)$
\Ensure Refined candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_e^{\star}$
\State \textbf{Branch 1: Ergodic random exploration (with probability $p_{\mathrm{rand}}$)}
\State \quad Uniformly sample a coordinate subset $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rand}}$ of size $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$;
\State \quad Invert binary coordinates in $\mathbf{x}_e$ over $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rand}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:ker_rand};
\State \textbf{Branch 2: Knowledge-guided directional refinement (with probability $1 - p_{\mathrm{rand}}$)}
\State \quad Calculate directional perturbation scores $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbed_score};
\State \quad Select top-$k_{\mathrm{flip}}$ candidate feature subset $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{know}}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:top_scores};
\State \quad Invert binary coordinates in $\mathbf{x}_e$ over $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{know}}$;
\State Evaluate mutant candidate fitness using wrapper classifier by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness};
\State Perform greedy pairwise in-place replacement according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:inplace};
\State \Return Refined elite candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_e^{\star}$;
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

At each generation, the top $k_e$ individuals ranked by objective fitness are admitted into the elite pool. For each elite candidate $\mathbf{x}_e \in \{0, 1\}^D$, the number of perturbed features is set to $k_{\mathrm{flip}} = \max\bigl(1, \lfloor \phi D \rfloor\bigr)$, where $\phi \in (0, 1)$ represents the perturbation fraction. As depicted in the operational schematic of Fig.~\ref{fig:ker_refinement}, the KER strategy probabilistically executes one of two operational pathways:

\textit{Branch~1: Ergodic random exploration}: To preserve search ergodicity and prevent the elite pool from stagnating in deceptive local basins, a feature index subset $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rand}} \subset \{1, \dots, D\}$ of cardinality $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$ is sampled uniformly at random with exploration probability $p_{\mathrm{rand}} \in [0, 1]$, and the corresponding coordinates are inverted:
\begin{equation}
x'_{e,j} \leftarrow 1 - x_{e,j}, \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rand}}, \quad |\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rand}}| = k_{\mathrm{flip}}.
\label{eq:ker_rand}
\end{equation}

\textit{Branch~2: Knowledge-guided directional refinement}: With complementary probability $1 - p_{\mathrm{rand}}$, the strategy executes targeted directional refinement driven by the cumulative importance landscape. For each candidate dimension $j \in \{1, \dots, D\}$, a state-dependent directional flip score $\sigma_j \in \mathbb{R}$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_j = (1 - 2 x_{e,j}) \cdot W_j = \begin{cases}
+W_j, & \text{if } x_{e,j} = 0, \\[2pt]
-W_j, & \text{if } x_{e,j} = 1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:flip_score}
\end{equation}
A positive flip score $\sigma_j > 0$ reflects two favorable search transitions: an unselected feature with inactive coordinate $x_{e,j} = 0$ that exhibits a positive historical contribution $W_j > 0$, or an active feature with $x_{e,j} = 1$ whose exclusion has historically improved classification fitness with $W_j < 0$. In either scenario, flipping the binary state of feature $j$ is expected to enhance candidate solution quality.

To balance directional guidance with exploratory diversity and resolve potential ties among identical scores, an adaptive stochastic perturbation $\xi_j \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, (\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{W}) + 10^{-5})^2\right)$ proportional to the empirical dispersion of the learned importance vector is injected into each directional score:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_j = (1 - 2 x_{e,j}) \cdot W_j + \xi_j, \quad \xi_j \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \left(\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{W}) + 10^{-5}\right)^2\right).
\label{eq:perturbed_score}
\end{equation}
The feature subset $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{know}}$ containing the $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$ coordinates with the largest perturbed directional scores is identified:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{know}} = \arg\max_{\substack{\mathcal{S} \subseteq \{1, \dots, D\} \\ |\mathcal{S}| = k_{\mathrm{flip}}}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}} \sigma_j,
\label{eq:top_scores}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding binary states are inverted via $x'_{e,j} \leftarrow 1 - x_{e,j}$ for all $j \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{know}}$.

\textit{Greedy pairwise in-place replacement}: The perturbed candidate $\mathbf{x}'_e$ is evaluated using the objective fitness function $f(\mathbf{x}'_e, \mathbf{w}(t))$. A greedy pairwise in-place replacement rule determines solution admission:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_e^{\star} = \begin{cases}
\mathbf{x}'_e, & \text{if } f(\mathbf{x}'_e, \mathbf{w}(t)) < f(\mathbf{x}_e, \mathbf{w}(t)), \\[2pt]
\mathbf{x}_e, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:inplace}
\end{equation}
Upon achieving a strict fitness improvement, $\mathbf{x}'_e$ immediately replaces $\mathbf{x}_e$ in the population and updates the global-best solution if $f(\mathbf{x}'_e, \mathbf{w}(t)) < f_{\mathrm{gbest}}$. Because the elite pool cardinality $k_e \ll N$ comprises only a minor fraction of the overall swarm, the KER strategy operates within a controlled computational overhead of $k_e$ fitness evaluations per generation. The complete procedural logic of elite refinement is formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ker}.

\textit{Dual-scale search dynamics and dimension-scaling analysis}: The perturbation budget $k_{\mathrm{flip}} = \max\bigl(1, \lfloor \phi D \rfloor\bigr)$ scales with ambient dimensionality $D$, producing distinct search behaviors across problem scales. In moderate-dimensional regimes where $D \le 2{,}000$, $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$ produces small-scale bit flips that execute fine-grained local neighborhood refinement around elite solutions. Conversely, in ultra-high-dimensional regimes where ambient dimensionality expands past $D > 10^4$, the perturbation budget scales proportionally into hundreds of simultaneous coordinate transitions, causing the KER strategy to shift dynamically from localized perturbation into a guided macro-recombination mechanism. Operating at this broader structural scale allows the swarm to evaluate coordinated multi-feature activations and deactivations guided by $\mathbf{W}$, facilitating transitions across distant combinatorial basins. Crucially, the greedy pairwise in-place replacement rule in \eqref{eq:inplace} serves as an intrinsic safeguard against destructive mutations: whenever an extensive macro-flip degrades classification accuracy or inflates feature penalties, the candidate mutant is discarded without modifying the elite pool. While fixed-fraction scaling $\lfloor \phi D \rfloor$ performs consistently across diverse benchmark scales, adaptively modulating the perturbation budget according to the active subset cardinality $d_{\mathrm{selected}} = \|\mathbf{x}_e\|_0$ via $k_{\mathrm{flip}} = \max\bigl(1, \lfloor \phi \cdot \|\mathbf{x}_e\|_0 \rfloor\bigr)$, or enforcing an upper saturation cap $k_{\max}$, represents an effective adaptive refinement for extreme-dimensional applications.

\subsection{Dynamic two-phase weight scheduling}\label{sec:twophase}

In ultra-high-dimensional wrapper feature selection where candidate dimensions $D \gg 10^3$ vastly exceed sample observations $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$, optimization must balance classification error against subset cardinality. Conventional metaheuristics rely on static scalarization, inducing severe search degeneration. A conservative feature penalty fails to overcome the dense dimensionality attractor, squandering evaluation budgets and precipitating classifier overfitting. Conversely, maintaining an elevated penalty throughout evolution suppresses late-stage convergence, prematurely discarding synergistic feature coalitions whose retention slightly increases cardinality but is vital for resolving complex decision boundaries.

To resolve this temporal conflict, KDS-EL introduces the dynamic TPS strategy, decoupling the search horizon into two functional phases, as depicted in the operational flowchart of Fig.~\ref{fig:kds_el_flowchart}. The total generation budget $T$ is partitioned at a split point parameterized by the phase transition ratio $\kappa \in (0, 1)$. The dynamic objective weight vector $\mathbf{w}(t) = [w_a(t), w_f(t)] \in [0, 1]^2$, satisfying the normalization condition $w_a(t) + w_f(t) = 1$, is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}(t) = \begin{cases}
[w_{a,1}, w_{f,1}], & 1 \le t \le \lfloor \kappa T \rfloor, \\[4pt]
[w_{a,2}, w_{f,2}], & \lfloor \kappa T \rfloor < t \le T,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:twophase}
\end{equation}
where the weight coefficients satisfy the strict ordering $w_{f,1} > w_{f,2}$ and $w_{a,1} < w_{a,2}$. At generation $t$, the unified scalar fitness $f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}(t))$ of a candidate binary mask $\mathbf{x} \in \{0, 1\}^D$ is evaluated as:
\begin{equation}
f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}(t)) = w_a(t) \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{Acc}(\mathbf{x})\bigr) + w_f(t) \cdot \left(\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_0}{D}\right),
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Acc}(\mathbf{x}) \in [0, 1]$ denotes the 5-fold cross-validation accuracy evaluated by a $k$-NN classifier with neighborhood parameter $k_{\mathrm{nn}} = 3$, and $\|\mathbf{x}\|_0 = \sum_{j=1}^D x_j$ quantifies the active feature cardinality.

The two evolutionary regimes execute complementary optimization roles. Throughout Phase~1, spanning $1 \le t \le \lfloor \kappa T \rfloor$, the elevated feature penalty $w_{f,1} = 0.30$ with $w_{a,1} = 0.70$ exerts substantial pruning pressure against non-informative features, rapidly contracting candidate dimensionality to a compact fraction of $D$. In Phase~2, spanning $\lfloor \kappa T \rfloor < t \le T$, weights shift to $w_{a,2} = 0.95$ and $w_{f,2} = 0.05$ under split ratio $\kappa = 0.70$, reorienting evolutionary pressure toward fine classification refinement. Because objective weights transition abruptly at generation $t = \lfloor \kappa T \rfloor + 1$, candidate fitness values operate on disparate scales. To mitigate scale distortion and preserve search continuity, all personal-best solutions are instantaneously re-evaluated under Phase~2 weights, setting $f_{\mathrm{pbest},i} \leftarrow f(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}, \mathbf{w}_2)$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, updating $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}$, and resetting stagnation counter $t_{\mathrm{stag}} \leftarrow 0$. This boundary calibration safeguards memory integrity, ensuring Phase~2 builds cohesively upon Phase~1 pruning without numerical distortion.

\subsection{Overall algorithmic procedure and complexity analysis}\label{sec:main_algo}\label{sec:complexity}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:kds_el} synthesizes the overall execution pipeline of KDS-EL into a unified multi-stage optimization framework. The complete workflow cohesively coordinates offline filter-level relevance extraction, knowledge-informed hybrid seeding, dynamic two-phase objective scheduling with boundary memory calibration, collaborative dual-subpopulation co-evolution, differential importance harvesting, targeted elite refinement, and adaptive stagnation recovery.

\begin{algorithm}[!htb]
\small
\caption{Overall algorithmic framework of KDS-EL}
\label{alg:kds_el}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Training dataset $\mathcal{D}$, population size $N$, maximum generations $T$, parameters $(\theta, \rho, \sigma, \gamma, k_e, \phi, p_{\mathrm{rand}}, \kappa, \tau)$
\Ensure Optimal compact feature subset $\mathbf{x}^{\star}$ and optimal fitness $f_{\mathrm{gbest}}$
\Statex \textbf{Stage 1: Filter-level relevance extraction}
\State Extract and normalize mutual information prior vector $\mathbf{m}$ using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mi_ksg} and~\eqref{eq:mi_norm};
\Statex \textbf{Stage 2: Hybrid population seeding}
\State Generate initial swarm $\mathcal{P}$ by combining MI-guided Gaussian seeds with uniform particles via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mi_seeding}--\eqref{eq:binarize};
\State Initialize importance vector $\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}$, initial weights $\mathbf{w}(1)$, and evaluate candidate fitness by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness};
\Statex \textbf{Stage 3: Dynamic two-phase co-evolutionary loop}
\For{$t = 1$ \textbf{to} $T$}
    \State Schedule dynamic objective weights $\mathbf{w}(t)$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:twophase};
    \State \textbf{if} $t = \lfloor \kappa T \rfloor + 1$ \textbf{then} calibrate personal and global bests under Phase~2 weights;
    \State Evolve Subpopulations $\mathcal{P}_1$ and $\mathcal{P}_2$ via collaborative multi-task search;
    \State Evaluate candidate solution fitness via 5-fold cross-validation by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness};
    \State Update cumulative importance vector $\mathbf{W}$ and apply memory decay $\gamma$ using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:importance_update}--\eqref{eq:decay};
    \State Refine top $k_e$ elite candidates using the KER strategy according to Algorithm~\ref{alg:ker};
    \State Update global best $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}$ and monitor swarm stagnation counter;
    \State \textbf{if} stagnation persists for $\tau$ generations \textbf{then} execute adaptive recovery by Eq.~\eqref{eq:reinit_formula};
\EndFor
\State \Return Optimal feature subset $\mathbf{x}^{\star} \leftarrow \mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}$ and optimal fitness $f_{\mathrm{gbest}}$;
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Computational complexity analysis}

The computational overhead of KDS-EL decouples into two distinct operational regimes: one-time offline preprocessing and online generational evolution. In the offline phase, evaluating non-parametric mutual information scores across $D$ candidate dimensions with $N_{\mathrm{samp}}$ training samples via $k$-nearest neighbors search requires $\mathcal{O}(D \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}} \log N_{\mathrm{samp}})$ operations, while normalizing and sorting these relevance estimates incurs $\mathcal{O}(D \log D)$ time. Because this procedure is executed exactly once prior to evolutionary search, the offline preprocessing complexity $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{pre}} = \mathcal{O}(D \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}} \log N_{\mathrm{samp}} + D \log D)$ represents a negligible fraction of the total budget, particularly in small-sample regimes where $N_{\mathrm{samp}} \ll 100$. Throughout online evolution, each generation $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ orchestrates three coordinated operational stages. First, subpopulation updates and cross-task knowledge transfer---comprising continuous velocity-position updates in $\mathcal{P}_1$ and discrete probabilistic sampling in $\mathcal{P}_2$---require $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot D)$ arithmetic operations per generation. Second, wrapper fitness evaluation constitutes the dominant computational expenditure: for each candidate solution $\mathbf{x}_i$ with active feature cardinality $d_i = \|\mathbf{x}_i\|_0$, computing pairwise Euclidean distances under 5-fold cross-validation with a $k$-NN classifier incurs $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 \cdot d_i)$ operations, aggregating to $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 \cdot \bar{d}^{(t)})$ across the swarm of size $N$, where $\bar{d}^{(t)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N d_i$ denotes the average active feature count at generation $t$. Third, cumulative importance learning and elite refinement introduce targeted search intensification within controlled overhead: extracting differential feature masks and updating memory vector $\mathbf{W}$ consumes $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot D)$ operations; computing directional flip scores across all $D$ dimensions and isolating top $k_{\mathrm{flip}}$ features incurs $\mathcal{O}(D \log D)$ time via partial quick-sort; and re-evaluating refined elite candidates requires $\mathcal{O}(k_e \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 \cdot d_{\mathrm{elite}})$ operations. Because the elite pool size is restricted to $k_e \ll N$, where $k_e = 3$, this refinement cost represents a marginal fraction of generational overhead. Accumulating these operational costs across $T$ generations yields the total asymptotic time complexity of KDS-EL:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{O}\left(T \cdot N \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 \cdot \bar{d}_{\mathrm{selected}} + T \cdot N \cdot D\right),
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{d}_{\mathrm{selected}}$ denotes the average active feature count across all evolutionary generations.

Although KDS-EL shares the identical worst-case asymptotic upper bound $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot N \cdot N_{\mathrm{samp}}^2 \cdot D)$ with canonical dual-swarm frameworks when expressed in terms of ambient dimensionality $D$, KDS-EL achieves an empirical 3.4-fold wall-clock acceleration, averaging 51.3~s compared to 175.0~s across the benchmark suite. This pronounced divergence between asymptotic complexity order and empirical execution latency stems directly from the cooperative interplay between prior-guided sparse initialization and dynamic Two-Phase Weight Scheduling. Rather than evaluating dense candidate masks where unguided swarms retain an average of over 2{,}700 features, KDS-EL aggressively contracts active feature cardinality to an ultra-sparse representation averaging merely 87.8 dimensions from early generations. Because computing pairwise Euclidean distances in the $k$-NN classifier scales strictly linearly with active feature count $d_{\mathrm{selected}}$, contracting selected dimensions by orders of magnitude directly slashes the actual floating-point distance calculations per wrapper evaluation, converting theoretical subset sparsity into pronounced real-world wall-clock acceleration.

Regarding spatial footprint, memory consumption comprises the binary population $\mathbf{X} \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times D}$, continuous positions $\mathbf{P} \in [0, 1]^{N \times D}$, velocities $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor \times D}$, personal bests $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{best}} \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times D}$, feature importance memory $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, and training data $\mathcal{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{samp}} \times D}$. The resulting total spatial complexity is $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{O}\left((3N + N_{\mathrm{samp}} + 1) D\right) = \mathcal{O}\left((N + N_{\mathrm{samp}}) D\right)$, scaling strictly linearly with feature dimensionality $D$. Because allocations utilize standard contiguous numerical arrays with small constant factors, working memory scales strictly linearly and remains modest even for high-dimensional problem instances with $D > 10^5$, ensuring computationally feasible execution within standard workstation memory budgets.

\subsubsection{Evaluation metrics and statistical test protocols}

Algorithm performance is evaluated across three primary quantitative criteria: classification accuracy with standard deviation, the number of selected features alongside the feature selection ratio, and computational runtime in seconds. A 3-nearest neighbor (3-NN) classifier with 5-fold stratified cross-validation serves as the wrapper evaluation model across all benchmark datasets, ensuring parameter-free reproducibility and high sensitivity to candidate feature subset discriminability. Following the standardized benchmarking protocol established in top-tier high-dimensional evolutionary feature selection literature~\cite{mel2024,wangjia2022,wei2025}, each benchmark problem is evaluated as a self-contained combinatorial optimization instance where cross-validation accuracy guides evolutionary search trajectories under an identical population size and generation budget across all competing methods.

To guarantee experimental fairness and statistical reliability, all algorithms are evaluated over 10 independent runs with distinct random seeds under the standardized parameter settings in Table~\ref{T3}. Following the established benchmarking protocol of peer evolutionary wrapper feature selection studies~\cite{mel2024,wangjia2022,wei2025}, each benchmark dataset is evaluated as a self-contained combinatorial optimization instance where an internal 5-fold stratified cross-validation wrapper, fixed with random seed 42, serves as the fitness evaluation surrogate across the 10 independent stochastic runs. Crucially, while iterative wrapper search against an internal cross-validation surrogate can introduce selection bias relative to completely unobserved external cohorts, this protocol guarantees exact fairness and numerical comparability across peer algorithms. The reported metrics thus quantify comparative combinatorial optimization efficacy under a shared benchmark standard, whereas prospective clinical translation strictly mandates outer nested cross-validation alongside independent multi-center cohort validation.

Furthermore, to determine whether the empirical advantages of KDS-EL over competing paradigms are statistically significant rather than stochastic artifacts, we adopt a two-tier non-parametric statistical evaluation framework. At the individual benchmark dataset level, the two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ test at the $\alpha = 0.05$ significance level evaluates performance differences across the 10 independent stochastic runs. Across the multi-problem benchmark suite, we strictly adhere to the non-parametric statistical testing framework recommended by Dem{\v{s}}ar~\cite{demsar2006}, where each problem is represented by its mean classification performance across the 10 independent runs. Specifically, the non-parametric Friedman test~\cite{friedman1940} establishes omnibus algorithm rankings across the benchmark suite, pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests assess directional superiority between KDS-EL and each competitor method, and the Holm--Bonferroni step-down procedure~\cite{holm1979} rigorously controls the family-wise error rate across multiple comparisons.

In contrast, conventional evolutionary feature selection algorithms exhibit severe dimensional redundancy. Even the most competitive peer wrapper, FWPSO~\cite{wangjia2022}, retains an average of over 1{,}000 features, while contemporary meta-heuristics such as bIRIME~\cite{su2024} preserve over 400 features due to the absence of targeted importance decay guidance. Most critically, canonical swarm heuristics, including BinCOA~\cite{shikoun2024}, DENCA-SO~\cite{hancer2023}, and standard BPSO~\cite{kennedy1997}, suffer from severe dimensional stagnation, selecting over 5{,}000 redundant features across high-dimensional landscapes. This degradation occurs because unguided binarization mechanisms inherently center active dimension probabilities around 0.50 when proactive pruning pressure is absent. By contrast, KDS-EL combines continuous mutual information prior seeding with aggressive early-stage feature penalization in Two-Phase Weight Scheduling, actively steering candidate trajectories into ultra-sparse, discriminative subspaces.

Evaluating the intermediate algorithmic configurations demonstrates how each constituent mechanism systematically compresses feature redundancy. Across the benchmark suite, feature cardinality contracts hierarchically from over 2{,}700 dimensions in the pre-improvement configuration down to fewer than 300 dimensions through mutual information prior seeding, further halves via knowledge-guided elite refinement, and ultimately consolidates into an ultra-compact signature of 87.8 features under dynamic two-phase scheduling. This progressive reduction confirms that the proposed components operate in a complementary cascade, where domain prior seeding suppresses global noise, elite refinement purges weakly contributing dimensions, and two-phase scheduling enforces rigorous dimensional parsimony.

Crucially, while feature reduction progresses monotonically in terms of the multi-dataset average, subset cardinality on individual problem instances reflects a deliberate, domain-specific trade-off governed by two-phase scheduling dynamics. On several challenging biomedical microarrays, such as \textit{Adenoma}, \textit{Lymphoma}, and \textit{SRBCT}, the complete KDS-EL framework retains slightly more features than the static-weight variant without TPS. This behavior directly manifests the underlying design rationale of the two-phase strategy: upon transitioning to Phase~2, the feature-cardinality penalty is relaxed while classification accuracy is prioritized. Rather than enforcing an inflexible sparsity constraint that risks permanently pruning synergistic feature interactions, this controlled relaxation empowers the search engine to strategically retrieve a small subset of weakly individual but strongly synergistic features that resolve ambiguous classification boundaries. Consequently, this targeted trade-off converts marginal feature retention into a statistically confirmed accuracy advantage, confirming that KDS-EL successfully harmonizes extreme feature sparsity with competitive predictive discrimination.

Crucially, despite executing slightly more function evaluations than standard swarm optimizers, KDS-EL achieves a 3.4-fold wall-clock acceleration. This empirical efficiency gain is primarily driven by active feature compression: in wrapper feature selection, pairwise Euclidean distance computations scale linearly with the active feature cardinality. By rapidly contracting candidate subsets from thousands of raw dimensions down to an average of 87.8 features within early generations, KDS-EL substantially diminishes per-evaluation distance latency compared to unguided swarms that retain over 2{,}700 dimensions. Concurrently, while the prolonged execution of bIRIME reflects a combination of larger feature subsets and higher candidate generation counts, KDS-EL restricts local refinement to a bounded elite pool, harmonizing thorough exploration depth with practical computational thrift.

\subsubsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:ceiling}

A critical empirical phenomenon in high-dimensional biomedical feature selection is the accuracy ceiling effect: across eight microarrays, classification accuracy exceeds 0.998 across leading optimizers. Under such near-perfect separation, accuracy differences narrow below 0.002, reflecting variations in at most a single sample. Consequently, classification accuracy ceases to provide sufficient discriminative resolution, making active feature cardinality paramount for assessing model parsimony~\cite{guyon2003,liang2025}.

Under this saturated regime, KDS-EL exhibits marked superiority in feature compactness, isolating ultra-sparse candidate feature subsets of only 39 to 83 dimensions across all eight ceiling benchmarks. In stark contrast, competing metaheuristics and evolutionary filters retain hundreds to thousands of redundant dimensions. This divergence highlights a pervasive failure mode: lacking early-stage dimensional penalization and elite refinement, optimizers succumb to feature bloat once high accuracy is attained. From a translational perspective, contracting candidate subsets to 40 to 80 informative genes on biomedical microarrays substantially scales down the hypothesis space into a tractable candidate regime for downstream experimental validation, whereas uncurated subsets spanning thousands of dimensions remain prohibitive for targeted screening.

Beyond nearest-neighbor metrics, a fundamental concern is inductive bias coupling~\cite{kohavi1997}: whether optimized subsets merely overfit the 3-NN surrogate. To examine cross-model discriminative transferability, compact subsets discovered by KDS-EL were transferred to Support Vector Machines and Random Forest classifiers. As documented in Supplementary Table S9, the discovered compact subsets exhibit favorable candidate-level cross-model consistency: on \textit{Adenoma} and the 12{,}625-dimensional \textit{ALL4} benchmark, contracting features by over 99.0\% elevates classification accuracy across all three learning paradigms, with all classifiers attaining 100\% accuracy on \textit{Adenoma} under merely 66 selected dimensions. On \textit{Colon}, the compact 14-feature signature substantially elevates 3-NN accuracy from 79.23\% to 88.59\%, while exhibiting slight performance attenuation from 85.51\% to 80.51\% under Support Vector Machines and from 85.38\% to 82.18\% under Random Forest. This attenuation naturally reflects the intrinsic divergence in inductive bias between instance-based metric learning relying on local Euclidean neighborhoods and margin-maximizing hyperplanes or orthogonal decision splits. Overall, the selected compact subsets maintain competitive discriminative efficacy across divergent model inductive biases, indicating that KDS-EL isolates informative candidate dimensions with cross-model consistency rather than purely algorithm-specific fitting artifacts.

Furthermore, within translational contexts, biological annotation of selected dimensions provides supportive evidence of concordance with established literature rather than de novo clinical biomarker discovery. Annotation of prominent candidate genes on representative microarray benchmarks reveals clear consistency with characterized oncological mechanisms, as detailed in Supplementary Table S10. In \textit{Colon} adenocarcinoma, identified dimensions show concordance with known molecular alterations of colorectal tissue: the selected subset includes \textit{COL11A1}, a documented marker of desmoplastic stroma and invasive carcinoma~\cite{fischer2001}; \textit{GUCA2A}, a mucosal differentiation hormone whose downregulation is an established hallmark of early colorectal neoplasia~\cite{alon1999}; alongside smooth muscle structural genes \textit{DES} and \textit{ACTG2} that delineate muscularis mucosae integrity. Similarly, in \textit{Prostate} carcinoma, recovered candidate genes include \textit{HPN} and \textit{AMACR}, widely documented markers in prostate pathology literature~\cite{singh2002,rubin2002}. This alignment serves as retrospective consistency evidence indicating that KDS-EL isolates genes with reported oncological relevance rather than arbitrary computational artifacts.

Finally, regarding the experimental evaluation methodology, following the standardized benchmarking protocol of peer evolutionary wrapper literature~\cite{mel2024,wangjia2022,wei2025}, each benchmark dataset is treated as a self-contained combinatorial optimization instance where an internal 5-fold stratified cross-validation wrapper, fixed with random seed 42, serves as the fitness evaluation surrogate across the 10 independent stochastic runs. This shared benchmark protocol guarantees exact empirical parity, reproducible search dynamics, and rigorous comparability of search thrift across all 14 evaluated optimizers. Crucially, while iterative wrapper optimization against an internal surrogate evaluates relative combinatorial search capability rather than completely unbiased external cohort generalization, prospective translational deployment of the computationally identified gene signatures strictly mandates outer nested cross-validation and independent multi-center cohort validation.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, KDS-EL is proposed to overcome search blindness, evolutionary amnesia, destructive perturbation, and static weighting bottlenecks in ultra-high-dimensional wrapper feature selection. By integrating mutual information prior initialization with adaptive recovery, KDS-EL establishes informative search basins while safeguarding diversity. To suppress destructive perturbations, the KER strategy harvests fitness differentials under exponential memory decay, steering targeted refinement within bounded computation. Furthermore, dynamic Two-Phase Weight Scheduling temporally decouples coarse dimensional pruning from fine classification refinement. Comprehensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that KDS-EL achieves competitive multi-criteria performance, attaining a 0.9545 mean benchmark-level cross-validation accuracy, contracting feature subsets to an average of 87.8 dimensions, and achieving a 3.4-fold empirical acceleration over canonical dual-swarm search. Beyond nearest-neighbor metrics, comparative evaluations confirm that the discovered compact feature subsets exhibit favorable candidate-level cross-model consistency across Support Vector Machines and Random Forest models under the benchmark wrapper protocol, while isolating compact candidate gene subsets of 39 to 83 dimensions that demonstrate retrospective concordance with genes with reported oncological relevance, including \textit{COL11A1}, \textit{GUCA2A}, \textit{HPN}, and \textit{AMACR}.

Several promising trajectories emerge for future exploration. First, integrating self-supervised embeddings could uncover higher-order epistatic interactions. Second, cross-cohort transfer mechanisms could enable reusing accumulated importance memories. Third, evaluating cross-run stability alongside multi-center cohort validation will advance computationally identified candidate signatures toward prospective clinical evaluation.
