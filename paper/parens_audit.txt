  Line: High-dimensional data have emerged as a fundamental challenge in classification problems across diverse domains, including genomics, text mining, and computer vision. These datasets typically contain hundreds or even tens of thousands of features, while only a limited number of labeled samples are available, resulting in the well-known \(p \gg n\) scenario. Under such conditions, conventional learning algorithms often suffer from severe overfitting, increased computational complexity, and reduced interpretability due to the curse of dimensionality~\cite{lili2017,guyon2003}. Feature selection (FS) has therefore become an essential preprocessing technique that aims to identify a compact and informative subset of features while preserving classification performance. However, selecting an optimal feature subset remains highly challenging because the search space grows exponentially with the number of features \(D\), and the underlying subset-selection problem is NP-hard~\cite{liang2025}. Moreover, individual feature relevance measures are insufficient to characterize complex feature interactions and dependencies, which are often critical for achieving robust classification performance.

sections\01_introduction.tex:5 -> (PSO)
  Line: FS methods are generally categorized into filter, wrapper, and embedded approaches~\cite{kohavi1997,lili2017}. Filter methods rank features according to statistical criteria and offer high computational efficiency, but they usually evaluate features independently of the classifier and may overlook feature interactions relevant to classification. Embedded methods incorporate feature selection into the learning process, providing a compromise between selection efficiency and predictive performance; however, their effectiveness is often dependent on the characteristics of specific learning models. Wrapper methods directly optimize feature subsets according to classifier performance and can achieve competitive classification results, but the repeated model evaluations make them computationally expensive, especially in high-dimensional search spaces. To address the combinatorial complexity of wrapper-based FS, evolutionary computation has been extensively investigated as a promising optimization paradigm~\cite{liang2025,kennedy1995,shi1998,kennedy1997}. By maintaining a population of candidate solutions, evolutionary algorithms can perform global exploration of feature subsets without requiring gradient information. Among them, particle swarm optimization (PSO), genetic algorithms, and differential evolution have demonstrated promising results on high-dimensional FS tasks.

sections\02_related_work.tex:51 -> (MOFS)
  Line: Feature selection naturally involves two conflicting objectives: minimizing classification error and minimizing the number of selected features. Multi-Objective Feature Selection (MOFS) algorithms model this trade-off by searching for a Pareto front of non-dominated solutions. Pioneered by Xue et al.~\cite{xue2012mopso}, diverse multi-objective evolutionary algorithms have been adapted to feature selection, including NSGA-II-FS~\cite{deb2002}, MOEA/D-FS~\cite{zhang2007}, MOPSO-FS~\cite{coello2004}, and hybrid filter-wrapper multi-objective architectures~\cite{hu2020,hancer2023}.

sections\02_related_work.tex:63 -> (EMTO)
  Line: Evolutionary Multi-Task Optimization (EMTO) aims to solve multiple distinct optimization tasks simultaneously by facilitating inter-task knowledge transfer through a unified chromosomal representation~\cite{zhang2021mtl}. sections\02_related_work.tex:65 -> (MEL)
  Line: Most notably, Zhang et al.~\cite{mel2024} introduced Multi-Task Evolutionary Learning (MEL), which establishes a dual-subpopulation PSO framework. In MEL, Subpopulation~1 performs continuous swarm exploration guided by inter-task attractors, while Subpopulation~2 conducts discrete feature sampling based on a shared feature-importance matrix. While MEL successfully demonstrates the power of cross-task knowledge transfer, its unguided multi-task formulation suffers from three critical bottlenecks: (i) random uniform initialization that causes unguided early-stage exploration in sparse spaces, (ii) unconstrained feature importance accumulation without memory discounting, and (iii) rigid static objective weights that delay early dimensionality reduction.

sections\03_preliminaries.tex:7 -> (PSO)
  Line: Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), originally introduced by Kennedy and Eberhart~\cite{kennedy1995}, is a population-based stochastic optimization paradigm inspired by the collective foraging behaviors of bird flocks. A swarm consists of $N$ particles navigating a $D$-dimensional search space $\mathbb{R}^D$. Each particle $i \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$ maintains a continuous position vector $\mathbf{p}_i^{(t)} = [p_{i,1}^{(t)}, \ldots, p_{i,D}^{(t)}]$ and a continuous velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_i^{(t)} = [v_{i,1}^{(t)}, \ldots, v_{i,D}^{(t)}]$ at iteration $t$. The historical trajectory is governed by its personal best position $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{best},i}$ and the swarm's global best position $\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{best}}$.

main.tex:198 -> (Knowledge-Guided Dual-Phase Swarm Evolutionary Learning)
  Line: Feature selection in high-dimensional data remains a central challenge for evolutionary computation. Particle swarm optimization suffers from three structural deficiencies: random initialization provides insufficient guidance in sparse high-dimensional spaces, uniform feature perturbation fails to exploit cross-iteration feature-importance signals, and static objective preferences cannot adapt to different evolutionary phases. To address these limitations, we propose KDS-EL (Knowledge-Guided Dual-Phase Swarm Evolutionary Learning), a knowledge-guided evolutionary framework with a closed-loop feature-importance learning mechanism. First, to overcome inefficient initialization, we propose mutual-information-guided initialization with adaptive re-initialization, injecting prior knowledge and recovering population diversity when stagnation occurs. Second, to exploit discarded cross-iteration feature-importance signals, we propose a knowledge-guided elite refinement strategy that accumulates feature-importance knowledge and steers elite solutions toward historically effective features. Third, to align objective weights with search phases, we propose two-phase weight scheduling that emphasizes early dimensionality reduction and late-stage accuracy refinement. On 20 high-dimensional benchmarks, KDS-EL achieves the best classification performance among all competitors, with a mean accuracy of 0.9574 and only 87.8 selected features, outperforming the strongest single- and multi-objective competitors by +3.97 and +4.42 percentage points; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm--Bonferroni correction confirm statistically significant improvements over all 13 competitors.\par
