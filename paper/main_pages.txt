Abstract

Feature selection in high-dimensional data, where the number of features vastly exceeds the number of samples, remains a central
challenge for evolutionary computation. Generic particle swarm optimization (PSO) suffers from three structural deficiencies in
this setting: random initialization wastes search budget in sparse solution spaces, uniform mutation discards cross-iteration feature-
importance signals, and fixed objective weights are misaligned with the distinct phases of the search. This paper proposes KDS-EL,
a knowledge-guided evolutionary learning method that builds upon a multi-task PSO backbone and integrates three complementary
mechanisms. Mutual-information-guided initialization with adaptive re-initialization supplies informed starting points and escapes
local optima on stagnation. A Knowledge-guided Elite Refinement (KER) strategy accumulates a cross-iteration feature-weight
matrix governed by exponential decay and flips elite solutions toward historically effective features at zero additional classifier
evaluations. A Two-Phase Weight Scheduling strategy partitions the search into an aggressive compression phase followed by
an accuracy-refinement phase. On 20 high-dimensional benchmark datasets spanning gene expression, text, image, and speech
domains, KDS-EL is compared against 13 competitors, including five single-objective PSO variants and five multi-objective feature
selection methods. KDS-EL ranks first with a mean accuracy of 0.9574 and only 87.8 selected features on average, outperforming
the strongest single-objective and multi-objective competitors by +3.97 and +4.42 percentage points, respectively, while delivering
a 3.4× speedup over the MEL backbone. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm superiority over MEL on 20/20 datasets, and Holm­
Bonferroni correction establishes statistical significance against all 13 competitors. The component contribution analysis shows that
the three mechanisms yield cumulative gains of +7.06 percentage points in accuracy and compress the feature count from 2,722 to
88.
